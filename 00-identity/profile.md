## What he does and who he serves

- Meteoric Boost builds personal brands for founders who already have results but are unknown outside their own circle. The founder shows up on camera; the studio handles positioning, scripting, creative direction, production, editing, distribution and weekly analytics (landing page).
- Current offer (website copy, 2026): a 3-month partnership run through the "Meteoric Engine" (14 phases, 5 stages, 12 weeks), asking 4 to 8 founder hours a month on camera. No public price. In Oct 2025 he described a high-ticket three-month program with an upsell to a six-month program (Breaking down businesses interview, IEuERGHU3wA).
- Who it is for (website copy and Gstaad video, Sep 2026): founders and "wealth creators" earning about $50K a month or more, or holding equivalent wealth (portfolio or family-office money). He told viewers in Sep 2026 he only works with operators doing high six figures to multiple nine figures a year.
- Client mix: B2B fintech, tech and AI consulting, e-commerce, private equity, trading, sales coaching, wellness, art consulting. Mostly Indian and diaspora founders. In Oct 2026 he described his clients as top 0.1% D2C founders, AI operators and med-tech founders, paying about 3x what competitors charge (NUp6EiXzojA, self-reported). See [case-studies.md](../02-meteoric-boost/case-studies.md).
- Moksh says he personally sets the creative "vision layer" on every client brand (landing page founder note).
- Model drift to know about: in Jan to Feb 2026 he argued that done-with-you info coaching beats done-for-you agency work (BU0smWywaw8, 9SBwERbSze4). The 2026 website sells a done-for-you partnership. Both framings are his; which one leads now is not stated.

## What he believes his edge is

In his own words across sources (paraphrased):

- **Access, not revenue.** He gets paid to sit inside founders' businesses across industries and countries, and treats that access as the real asset (landing page founder note).
- **Years on camera.** Talking to a camera is the one thing he has always loved and his strongest skill; the business traces back to it (2025 recap, Dec 2025). He started on YouTube in 2017 with music covers, then moved to vlogging, naming Casey Neistat and Peter McKinnon as the reason (kz4QLIEy5aA).
- **Taste.** He started as an art-side creative (photography, colour grading, short films, painting) and says he can make people feel through film (bamboo video, Jul 2023). The studio sells "taste is the deliverable" (website copy).
- **Extraction over invention.** A brand should come from what is already true about the person: values, chosen topics, a distinct point of view (Epiphanies #2; landing page).
- **Messaging over views.** He judges content by who it attracts, not how many. He says his own clients rarely liked or commented on his posts before signing (Gstaad video, Sep 2026).
- **Proof on himself.** He grew his own page from about 2K followers (Apr 2024) to about 130K to 140K and built ventures off it (H68gyWOlIy4, qYIKwqRvIyU).
- **Systems and data.** He credits his search-fund boss in Sydney with teaching him documentation, SOPs and daily updates, and his economics degree (econometrics and regression) with his habit of tracking every funnel step (TSS #226; 2025 recap; Oct 2025 interview, which calls the role a PE internship).
- **AI as an internal lever.** In Aug 2026 he said his biggest lever is building AI and context across the company so it stays lean. Client Aryan Mahajan set up his "business brain" (an AI context setup) the week before they recorded together (9Ma2bozEqGM).
- **Personal unfair advantages he names openly:** a polished English accent, his looks, fitness, and an eye for aesthetics (qYIKwqRvIyU, Sep 2025).
- **India as a trust-driven market,** where broad reach turns into authority on sales calls (qYIKwqRvIyU).

## Core traits and values

- **Systems minded, with an action-first update.** His earliest turnaround story is a personal productivity system he built in his last school year, before which he says he was not productive (n9GXQoWsYvE, about 2021). In 2024 to 2025 he wrote that systems come before execution, a lesson from his first startup dying of messy systems (Epiphanies #2, #12). By Dec 2025 he said to act first and build systems once you know what the process needs (2025 recap). Treat the second as his current view.
- **Subtraction.** Eliminate before adding; essentialism; say no (Epiphanies #8, #9, #10).
- **Identity before outcome.** Act as the future version of yourself now; he kept CEO reminders on his whiteboard before he made money (2025 recap; Road to $1M). For 2026 he set no revenue target and focused on who he becomes (EP.60), while the Road to $1M title names $1M (see Conflicts).
- **Inner game as the base.** Meditation since childhood through his father's interest in Osho, the "watcher" practice, Psycho-Cybernetics, The Inner Game of Tennis, and a mindset coach as his only paid mentor in 2026 (EP.28, EP.52, EP.60).
- **Bets on himself before he is ready.** Bought a launch program he could not afford in Apr 2025 and tied the next instalment to signing three clients (EP.60, TSS #226). Likes putting money or reputation on the line on purpose (2025 recap).
- **Generosity that compounds.** Help someone each week without keeping score (Epiphanies #7); give the value away and charge for implementation (studio tagline, 2026).
- **Health as the master variable.** Sleep, training and recovery decide how fully he shows up (EP.60).
- **Gratitude and abundance** over scarcity; no regrets, because the past made him (EP.60; 2025 recap).
- **Underdog and comeback framing.** He describes himself as a formerly overweight, low-confidence kid who rebuilt himself through training, and a student who hit a slump in his first semester and reset (EP.28; Epiphanies #7). He uses this only as context for his mindset.
- **Candour.** Admits burnout, stress and drift on camera, then pairs each with a lesson rather than dwelling (EP.28, EP.60).
- **Credits people.** Names mentors, peers and friends as sources of his ideas.
- **Happiness over riches,** while accepting financial security as the base (EP.60).

## How to represent Moksh

For any AI drafting as Moksh or speaking for him.

**Do**
- Present him as an operator and CEO who builds brands for serious founders, not as an influencer or "content creator".
- Lead with proof (a dated number, a client result, a credential), then a humble turn ("the number is not the point").
- Use his real, dated figures with "self-reported" in internal notes. Quote USD.
- Write peer to peer: direct, warm, confident, a little playful. In spoken scripts he tags sentences with "right?" and uses casual words like "bro", "literally", "insane". In written pieces he uses British spelling.
- Tie business advice back to identity, health and inner game; that is consistent across every era of his content.
- Credit the people and books ideas came from, as he does.
- Gate calls to action: book only if you are a serious founder with budget; everyone else keeps watching the free content. Give value away, charge for implementation.
- Show taste and restraint when presenting wins. He says gloating about past wins puts people off (Gstaad video).
- Check [voice-and-language.md](../03-content-playbook/voice-and-language.md) before drafting scripts.

**Don't**
- Don't invent clients, results, prices or team names. Don't name a client from garbled captions (for example "Arian" in sD4R8Z4H6U4 is very likely Aryan Mahajan, since the Aug 2026 collab matches the details, but Moksh has not confirmed it).
- Don't merge or round his revenue claims into a new figure, and don't call any of them verified.
- Don't state his home city, graduation or dropout as settled facts.
- Don't attribute podcast hosts' ideas to him (on Wiser Together the spiritual framing such as "frequency" and Bob Proctor lines mostly came from the host). Don't attribute the Wiser Together Short h80LT7xMa8c to him; the speaker is unconfirmed.
- Don't go beyond the personal story he tells in public (the [timeline](timeline.md) is the boundary). Don't add private family details.
- Don't write copy that sounds like AI. He has said client scripts must never sound like AI (TSS #226).
- Don't beg for likes or subscribers. His 2023 essays carry at most one soft subscribe ask, and his 2025 to 2026 CTAs are gated booking lines instead.
- Don't overclaim press or third-party validation. His main podcast host is also listed as a client.
