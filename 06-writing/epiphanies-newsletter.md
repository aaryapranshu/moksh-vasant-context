### The content system he shared in #15

This is the clearest written description of his personal production system and the root of the later Meteoric Engine ([meteoric-engine.md](../02-meteoric-boost/meteoric-engine.md)):

1. Journal nightly and weekly.
2. Spot recurring themes and pull out 5 to 6 topics.
3. Brain-dump each topic, then shape it into a script with a hook, body and outro.
4. Batch-record 6 to 7 voiceovers and add music.
5. Hand editing and distribution to the team through Notion.
6. One weekly review call.

He put the total at about 2 to 3 hours a month for about 12 reels.

## Written voice and structure

Use this section to draft a new issue that reads like his. Pair it with [voice-and-language.md](../03-content-playbook/voice-and-language.md) for his spoken voice.

### Register

- Warm, first-person, reflective. A weekly journal shared with friends, not a lecture.
- Positions himself as a learner documenting what he is working out, while still giving clear frameworks.
- Candid about stress, burnout, emotion and setbacks, always paired with what he took from them.
- Light profanity ("WTF", "goddamn"), Indian-English touches ("lakhs"), cricket and F1 metaphors, finance terms explained plainly.
- Patriotic India framing appears near the end of the run (the "Jai Hind" sign-off in #15).

### Fixed skeleton

1. **Opener.** A greeting ("Hey," / "Hey, you." / "What's good people" / "grab a coffee") or a time-elapsed recap ("two weeks since...", "Nine days.").
2. **Life update.** What happened since the last issue: travel, work, sport, a specific moment. Concrete places and numbers.
3. **One or two numbered "Epiphany" sections.** Each names a lesson, tells the story behind it, then gives a short framework or rule. Often closes on a standalone emphasis line.
4. **Resources.** A short list under a heading like "Worth your time", "Current Reads" or "Recommendations": books, newsletters, interviews, with one line each on why.
5. **CTA.** Give first: a free or discounted Notion template with a 24 to 48 hour window, a resource, "reply to me", or a nudge to follow a project. Never a hard ask for a call.
6. **Sign-off.** Nearly always "Until next time," followed by his bolded name. Variants: "Speak soon", "More soon", "Thanks for being here".

### Formatting habits

- H3 headings, bold key phrases, bullets and numbered frameworks, arrow (→) lists.
- Short standalone lines for emphasis (for example "Eliminate > Add").
- Few emojis: usually 0 to 1 per issue (more in #13).
- Image-heavy with jokey photo captions.
- Length: about 600 to 1,400 words, usually 650 to 1,000. Some issues carry a reading-time subtitle ("A 4 minute read").
- British spelling (realise, prioritise, maximising).
- Lowercase "i" in titles and subtitles ("how i made it happen", "Where do i begin..."), standard casing in the body. This matches his YouTube titles.
- His originals used dashes freely. Drafts for him should use commas, colons, full stops or parentheses instead.

### Drafting a new issue: checklist

- Pick one real recent event from Moksh's week (only from material he provides; never invent one).
- Pull one or two lessons from it and number them as epiphanies.
- Give each lesson a concrete story, a number where there is one, and a one-line rule.
- Add 2 to 4 resources with one honest line each.
- End with a give-first CTA, then the sign-off.
- Keep it under about 1,000 words, warm and specific, with no AI filler.

### Era note for 2026 drafts

The newsletter belongs to his student-founder period. Since mid-2025 his public writing has moved to premium, selective positioning (studio site copy, "for millionaires" video titles, a $50K-a-month client floor). A restarted newsletter would likely need to blend the diary format above with his current CEO and studio positioning. How he wants that balance struck is a question for Moksh, not something to assume.
