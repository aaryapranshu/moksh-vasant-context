## Core thesis
Accomplished founders lose introductions, invites and deals because a cold visitor can't decode their profile in about three seconds. The fix is legibility, not more posting. Every surface of the profile (picture, bio, highlights, pinned posts, grid) should answer one of the visitor's questions or remove one hesitation, all aimed at the same high-caliber person. Done well, the profile introduces you to a room before you walk in.

## Walkthrough

### 1. Hook: three seconds [00:00]
- "You get literally 3 seconds when a cold person lands on your Instagram profile and that's your entire first impression" [00:00].
- The goal: turn the profile into "a high authority asset" [00:07]. Everything downstream ("the introductions, the rooms, the first conversation, the deals") "resolves from that one moment" [00:20].
- Credentials: "I have outpositioned the entire market" and "I've built this for 50 plus founders across Los Angeles, Dubai, Mumbai, London" [00:32]. His profile has "attracted the top 0.1% of founders" to Meteoric Boost.

### 2. Diagnosis: credible but illegible [00:45]
- "you're the most credible person in the room, but the least legible" [00:45] (captions add "outsidered").
- You think you need to post more or better, but you don't, until this is fixed. A cold visitor "cannot tell in seconds who you are, what you do, or who it is for" [00:59], so they leave with no impression, "which is worse than having a bad impression" [00:59].
- People with "a fraction of your credibility get the podcast, the invite, the intro, the deal" because they are legible [01:12].
- The typical case: "Your LinkedIn has a 9 figure network" but "Your Instagram has 500 followers and no answer to who is this" [01:12 to 01:24].
- The fix: make the profile "a modern-day landing page" [01:37].

### 3. The framework: the 3-second profile [01:37]
- A cold visitor "asks four questions in this exact sequence and every surface exists to answer" them [01:50].
- He does not list the four in one place. The questions he names as he goes are: who are you, what do you do, who is it for [00:59, 02:53, 03:20], and, at the highlights, "is this built for someone like me?" [04:46]. (The earlier version of this file listed the four as one block; that is a reconstruction.)
- He promises his own profile plus "a couple of examples of what we've done for clients" [02:02].

### 4. Profile picture [02:02]
- "clean, high authority profile picture where your face is visible. The image is zoomed in and you're in good lighting" [02:16].
- He changes his "almost three to four times every single year", matched to the season: summer means greens, blues and a daytime shot; colder months go warmer, darker, maybe a night shot [02:16 to 02:29].
- "you don't need to go that in depth" [02:41]. Zoomed in, face visible, good light is what matters.

### 5. Bio [02:53]
- **Who:** "founder and CEO of @meteoric.boost" [03:07].
- **What's next:** "soon @[events company]", captioned as "Asava event": "I'm building this events company with my business partner" [03:07 to 03:20]. Name unconfirmed.
- **What he does (tagline):** "I work with founders to build the top 0.1% personal brands to inspire movements" [03:20].
- **Link:** a video sales letter that pre-sells a founder and "redirects the person to my YouTube", so they go "down a rabbit hole" of his content [03:33 to 04:00].
- **Flexibility:** the link can be the company website, LinkedIn or X; "whatever is the thing or the asset that explains exactly what you do" [04:11]. One client has his LinkedIn there, one has no link, another has a link; it depends on "what's the highest trust asset" [04:21 to 04:33].

### 6. Highlights: "is this built for someone like me?" [04:46]
- "Every highlight closes one hesitation before it's spoken" [04:58]. He is "trying to convert them at every single touch point" without being salesy, so the visitor ends up thinking "I need to work with this guy" [05:11].

### Profile audit checklist (built from this video)
- [ ] A cold visitor can tell who you are, what you do and who it is for within about three seconds.
- [ ] Profile picture: zoomed in, good light, face clearly visible. Optional: refresh it to the season (he does three to four times a year).
- [ ] Bio: role and company tag, what's next (if relevant), a one-line tagline of what you do, and a link to your highest-trust asset (pre-sell video, site, LinkedIn or X).
- [ ] Highlights answer "is this built for someone like me?": life, thoughts or mental models, an ICP-targeted set of stories with client results, a "new here" intro of about 10 to 15 stories, and client proof.
- [ ] Each highlight closes a specific hesitation.
- [ ] Three pinned posts (video preferred, carousels acceptable): clients raving, team and culture, a personal intro.
- [ ] The grid follows one core theme chosen for this person (his is motion; don't copy it).
- [ ] The grid's taste reflects the quality of the work.
- [ ] No surface contradicts another. All of them target the same caliber of person.
- [ ] The company page is proof-heavy and links to long-form testimonial interviews (10 to 15 minutes).
- [ ] The CTA is a qualifying call, not an open invitation.

## Script anatomy
- **Hook [00:00]:** a stat (three seconds) and the stakes (introductions, rooms, deals), then credentials (50+ founders, four cities, the top 0.1%).
- **Problem [00:45]:** the credible-but-illegible diagnosis, made concrete with a 9-figure LinkedIn network next to a 500-follower Instagram.
- **Framework [01:37]:** names "the 3-second profile" and its four questions.
- **Demo [02:02 to 13:53]:** top to bottom: picture, bio, highlights, pins (each pinned video played), grid. Client examples mixed in.
- **Synthesis [14:06]:** one caliber, no contradictions.
- **Second demo [14:19]:** the company page as "proof max".
- **Contrast [15:44]:** "the difference between you and me", introduced to the room before you arrive.
- **CTA [17:07]:** a strategy call with "my team", framed as a fit filter, "not for beginners".
- **Retention:** each profile element is a self-contained segment (easy to clip); embedded testimonial and team clips break up the screen-share.
- **Mode:** a teaching breakdown that ends on a call, unlike his story-led travel monologues.
