# AI & Personal branding is a 9 figure play.. @AryanXMahajan

A two-hour remote conversation between Moksh and his client and friend Aryan Mahajan (LA-based AI architect) covering how they met, both of their origin stories, nonlinear growth, money, validation, and how AI changes the way a lean company runs.

## Quick facts
- Moksh records from Gujarat (he mentions the internet there being unreliable); Aryan records from LA at around 5am. At one point Aryan refers to Moksh being in Mumbai, which adds to the existing home-city conflict (see OPEN-QUESTIONS).
- Both speakers are about 22.
- The captions carry no speaker labels. Attributions below come from self-identifying cues (Sydney, calisthenics and Gujarat point to Moksh; Dubai, Upwork, LinkedIn and LA point to Aryan). Where a pass disagreed, it is flagged.
- Timestamps are not in the captions. All positions below are estimates from where a passage falls in the text, mapped to the 2:13:14 runtime.
- The "9 figure play" thesis appears only in the title. Neither speaker argues it on air in the available text. Treat it as packaging.

## Core thesis
AI and personal branding feed each other: the personal brand brings in trust and inbound deals, and AI lets a small team run the business behind it. Underneath that, both speakers argue that growth is nonlinear when you reposition toward more sophisticated buyers, that safety nets slow you down, and that founders have to stop needing outside validation.

## Who said what

### Moksh (host)
- Frames work as play and says he and Aryan live a 360-degree life where work and life blur; a personal brand is part of the business, not separate from it.
- Says Aryan's Fortune 500 deals come from Aryan's LinkedIn personal brand, and calls their relationship symbiotic: Moksh's brand work and Aryan's AI work feed each other.
- Credits Aryan with teaching him context engineering, how to structure Claude Projects (auto-captioned as cloud projects), and treating AI as a living, breathing system. Says Aryan set up his business brain the week before recording and calls it impressive.
- Says his biggest lever is engineering AI and context across his company so the business gains its own market insight, and that his goal is a lean company with AI as the main lever.
- Argues growth only looks linear until you reposition toward a more sophisticated ICP, even if that costs short-term business; he calls this transurfing realities (his signature phrase, see Voice notes) and says most competitors still think in straight lines.
- Says seemingly unrelated experiences compound: his university career funnel and cold outreach led to the private equity internship, which taught him to operate and lead a team, which fed Meteoric.
- Money stance: he says he has a spiritual connection with money, sees it as compounding value and a game, and argues that perceived value drives the first purchase while delivery drives retention. Price follows the client's gain, so a Fortune 500 company pays more than a local business for similar work.
- Validation: about 150 people congratulated him on his Sydney graduation, while business milestones drew almost no one. He was already running Meteoric at about $20k months by then (self-reported). He asks Aryan how many people congratulated him on his first six-figure month; Aryan says nobody. Moksh's lesson: founders must let go of needing validation.
- Says Aryan was at about $80k a month when they started working together (Moksh's figure about Aryan's business).
- Says networking events are mostly performative; he cold-emailed about 10 people a day instead.

### Aryan Mahajan (guest)
- Hired Moksh to build his Instagram brand. He had traction on LinkedIn and Twitter but found Instagram a different game (more visual, more story-driven). He used AI to research people in the personal-branding space, took several calls, and picked Moksh as the only one with a strong personal brand of his own.
- Says Instagram is a networking tool in LA: the first thing people exchange there is their Instagram handle.
- Runs AI infrastructure work for Fortune 500s, hedge funds and ecommerce brands, mostly selling to CEOs and the C-suite. Says most executives are stuck using chatbots, and his job is structuring AI so it moves revenue and margins. Deals roughly $10k to $100k+. Has done this for about 1.5 years, with LinkedIn content as the main acquisition channel.
- Says selling AI to B2B is hard, and many people selling AI courses are not earning real client money themselves.
- Says he has had six-figure months (around late 2025 or early 2026) but no longer talks about revenue publicly.
- Money stance: money is a tool for making bigger moves and a byproduct of chasing greatness; he does not see it as spiritual. He funds small experiments (for example a data-center venture with a partner) to learn a market quickly.
- Left a remote job to remove his safety net; says no plan B forced him to make money.
- Says his staff is effectively 50 AI agents running around the clock, at roughly $200 a month in Claude and Codex subscriptions (said half as a joke).
- Splits a business into a mechanical side (data, SOPs, bottlenecks, ad attribution, market research), which AI can largely automate, and a human side (taste, curation, marketing, sales), which should stay human-led with AI as an accelerator. Example: AI can spot winning ad copy but cannot invent new ad concepts. (Attribution to Aryan is from context; one pass assigned this to Moksh.)
- Predicts white-collar work, especially software engineering, is being disrupted before blue-collar work. Cites a Twitter co-founder who reportedly cut 40% to 50% of a new company's staff.
- Critiques n8n's marketing and says AI tools go stale fast. Says new Claude users dump raw documents into Projects without structure.
- Lead magnets: his first enterprise client came from a LinkedIn lead magnet; one lead magnet drew about 10,000 comments (attribution likely Aryan). A lead magnet only converts if the positioning and the resource are both strong.

### Both
- The early phase of a business is lonely; friends and family may disapprove, the social circle shrinks, and better connections arrive once you have leverage.
- Both do their highest-impact work late at night (around 11pm to midnight); daytime goes to clients and team. Which speaker leads this point is unclear across passes.
- Some close relationships get lost when you make bold moves; the last couple of years taught them who they are and which dangled offers to turn down (low confidence on who says what).
- Fitness is a non-negotiable discipline that carries into business.
