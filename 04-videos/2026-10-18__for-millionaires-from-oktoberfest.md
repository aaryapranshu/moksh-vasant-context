# for millionaires, from oktoberfest (your personal brand sucks)
Moksh argues that a founder brand moves you out of price competition into trust competition, then breaks down the parts of his own brand that pull in top founders: repositioned audience, case studies, a visible team, a curated look, evergreen assets, and a luxury-grade client experience.

## Core thesis
AI and cheap tools make products easy to copy, so most businesses end up competing on price and quality. A founder brand is the way out: people buy from the person they trust, pay more for it, and treat the relationship like a status good. His own brand is the live example.

## Walkthrough
1. **Hook:** once you build a brand around yourself rather than only the business, you can charge premium prices because clients are buying into you. Promises to break down how his own brand attracts top founders.
2. **Two games:** commoditization (AI makes launching apps and companies easy, so everyone fights on price and quality) versus differentiation (people choose you on trust). Says he himself would buy from a tech operator whose content he has followed.
3. **Proof of the trust game:** Meteoric works with what he calls the top 0.1% of founders: D2C founders with large revenue, AI operators earning hundreds of thousands a month, med-tech founders. The agency moved up market through trust, not volume. Clients pay roughly three times what competitors charge and care more about a curated network than follower counts.
4. **Brand as status symbol:** compares a personal brand to a Rolex or quiet luxury. Clients buy reliability and something they can show peers, and then introduce him to their own networks.
5. **Ingredients of his brand:**
   - Follower growth, then repositioning: grew to about 140k followers between 2023 and 2025, then repositioned toward business goals by showing past work, audience outcomes, and investor interest.
   - Case studies: client testimonials and case studies run in front-end marketing and bring in more founders. Example: an Indian marketing agency founder at about $150k MRR onboarded the day of filming.
   - Showing the team: features his head of operations, creative director, and conversion team so prospects trust the people before onboarding. Team members post their own content, which builds rapport. He says whether to do this depends on the business.
   - The two-day research window: before reaching out, prospects check YouTube, Instagram, company pages, LinkedIn, and more. Make that window convert.
   - Visual taste: films in tasteful locations and keeps pages clean and curated. Cites a founder who said he would not have signed without seeing Moksh's Monaco content (referred to as an ad).
   - Angles versus assets: angles are themes (like this video's topic); assets are evergreen pieces, such as YouTube videos, that keep converting.
6. **Client experience as luxury:** defines luxury as the feeling of being taken care of. Examples: business-class flights, a custom-engraved perfume bottle, a relationship with a Rolex store, cashmere blankets at a Stuttgart dinner. Meteoric aims for that feeling from the first WhatsApp message to the final testimonial shoot, including flying the team to clients' cities.
7. **Close:** busy people want to be taken care of. No hard CTA; the implied pitch is to work with Meteoric.
