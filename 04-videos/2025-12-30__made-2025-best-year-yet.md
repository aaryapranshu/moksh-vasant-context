# How i made 2025 my best year yet (hear me out...)
Moksh's year-in-review, filmed in his birthday week just after turning 22: twelve principles from his journal about action, proximity, sprints, identity, team and data, each told with a personal story and a hard number.

## Core thesis
His 21st year was his best yet, and he credits a stack of principles from his journal: act before you systematise, get around people far ahead of you, drop excuses, sprint hard and then rest, run on gratitude and identity instead of scarcity and goals, build a lean team people feel they own, and double down on whatever the data says works. Underneath it: the results are 5 to 6 years of compounding effort that began with talking to a camera in 2017.

## Walkthrough
### Hook: credential stack (approx. 0:00 to 1:00)
- Name, age 22, founder and CEO of Meteoric Boost, which he describes as an agency helping founders and "wealth creators" build and monetise personal brands.
- Proof (self-reported): about 143k Instagram followers that he has monetised; about $110k lifetime revenue from a new offer launched about five months earlier; 8 years of making videos since 2017, which he calls his strongest skill.
- Humble pivot: the numbers are not the point; it all started from the one thing he has always loved, talking straight to a camera.
- Frames the video as a reflection on his 21st year, filmed in his birthday week.

### Setting and "not bragging" (approx. 1:00 to 2:10)
- Points out Dubai Marina, the yachts, the cigar, and says the intro is context, not a flex.
- Calls the year transformational: heavy travel, varied work environments, clients older, richer and more experienced than him.
- Credits compounding effort over 5 to 6 years. Says the list comes from his journal and is meant for people ahead of him and people just starting.

### 1. Action beats everything (approx. 2:10 to 4:10)
- Fast, imperfect action drives progress; systems come after you have acted and learned what the process needs.
- "Overleveraging": deliberately put money or reputation on the line so it becomes do-or-die.
- Effort is not hours logged; you work until it is done, even if that means brutal sprints near a deadline.

### 2. Get your reality shattered (approx. 4:10 to 7:10)
- Regularly get into rooms with people far ahead so your baseline resets upward. Mentions meeting founders doing $50k to $300k a month across SEO, info products, SaaS and e-commerce.
- Points at a yacht he estimates is worth several times his net worth; discloses last month's income as about $31k.
- Dubai trip story: the group spent about $10k on supercars and experiences; he scratched a Ferrari rim and paid about $6k in fines. Uses it to show how fast money goes and how that pushes him to earn more.
- Confidence with a bit of ego is useful; comparing yourself to top performers pulls you toward their level.

### 3. No crutches (approx. 7:10 to 9:00)
- Stop using circumstances as excuses; aim to be strong in every domain of life.
- Nuance: being a jack of all trades is fine eventually, but early on you need focus. Stick to one business model until it works, then expand with capital, network and labour.
- Threshold example: once the business hits about $10k a month, start prioritising health and relationships. He admits he deferred them when he had no money; wealth removes that excuse.

### 4. Everything comes to you (approx. 9:00 to 11:00)
- Operate from the energy you want to receive; act as if the outcome is already yours. He says he expected a strong team, top client results and clients paying in full without objections, and that this is now partly true.
- Anecdote: at a Mumbai rooftop bar where he was consistently kind to staff, a waiter comped his bill because he came often and his birthday was near. Lesson: kindness without a selfish motive creates reciprocity.

### 5. Work and rest like a lion (approx. 11:00 to 12:50)
- **40-day sprints:** intense focus with health, sleep and daily meditation dialled in, plus visualising his future self.
- Daily load of 4 to 8 hours across sales calls, client calls, strategy, content, systems and team calls, allocated to wherever the business bottleneck is.
- Between sprints: 10 to 20 days of travel, socialising and perspective. Grinding 60 to 180 days straight is counterproductive.
- Claims the sprints multiplied his results 2 to 4x (self-reported).

### 6. Gratitude and abundance (approx. 12:50 to 14:40)
- Run on gratitude, not scarcity; jealousy and fear come from scarcity thinking. Losses like the fine become lessons.
- Admits some of his drive comes from insecurity and pain, and tries to be grateful for that pressure.
- Life is short, so he wants it full of experiences, relationships and impact. Loses track of his numbering here and laughs it off.

### 7. Expand horizons, get it out of your system (approx. 14:40 to 16:50)
- Once early goals are hit, spend some money on experiences so you feel the hedonic treadmill yourself.
- Caveat: build an emergency fund first. He acknowledges having a safety net that let him spend more freely.
- Realisation: purchases give short bursts of happiness; gratitude and experiences last. Knowing it and living it are different.

### 8. Don't chase goals, embody the identity (approx. 16:50 to 18:20)
- Ask who your future self is and act like that person today. His example: behaving like a $50k or $100k a month entrepreneur before the revenue arrives.
- Method: a **vision diary** mapping the next few months, visualised daily while feeling it as already real.

### 9. Ruthlessness, killer instinct (approx. 18:20 to 20:30)
- Ruthless means decisive and competitive in status games (business, dating, purchases), not cruel and not wanting to harm anyone.
- Calls himself spiritual, not religious. Draws on Lord Shiva as an archetype of detached, fierce energy; wears a mala as a reminder; chants and meditates.
- Applies it on sales calls: pressing on pain points and quoting high prices.

### 10. Build an insane lean team and culture (approx. 20:30 to 22:00)
- Four department heads with him as CEO over them.
- Lead by example as the hardest worker. Hire for culture fit and intuition over raw skill, through a rigorous application. Skills can be taught; ownership mindset cannot.
- Says he paid the team beyond what revenue justified during slow periods, including times he technically could not afford it.
- Wants the team to feel the company is theirs.

### 11. Real connections with the team (approx. 22:00 to 23:00)
- Flew the team to Mumbai, where they surprised him in his hometown. Next year's plans include team trips to Bali and Cape Town.
- People as obsessed as you make work more fun; visible obsession attracts collaborators and clients.

### 12. Double down on what works (approx. 23:00 to 25:30)
- Studied economics at university and found econometrics and data analysis especially useful.
- Company split into four functions: sales, operations, fulfilment, marketing.
- Track data for every department and step, form hypotheses about why numbers are high or low, find what works, then pour time, capital, labour, systems and throughput into it without embarrassment.

### Close (approx. 25:30 to end)
- Says filming this reminded him why he fell in love with making videos; he is grateful.
- Promises a separate video on the last 1.5 to 2 years (open loop).
- Thanks viewers who have followed since 2017, welcomes new ones, invites them back. Casual sign-off. No lead magnet, no booking ask, no subscribe plea.

## Script anatomy
- **Hook:** identity and credential stack in about 60 seconds (age, title, ICP, followers, revenue plus timeframe, years on camera), then the humble pivot, then the sensory setting (Dubai, yachts, cigar, birthday), then a "not bragging" disclaimer, then a promise of value for two audiences (people ahead of him and beginners).
- **Credibility move:** numbers inside the first minute, reinforced by the luxury setting as visual proof.
- **Structure:** numbered listicle of 12 principles. Each one: name the principle, give the lesson, add a personal story with a hard number, then add a caveat (emergency fund first; ruthless but not cruel).
- **Retention devices:** countable list, dollar figures in nearly every section, vivid stories (Ferrari rim, rooftop waiter, yacht), tonal shifts from business to spirituality, self-aware lost-count moments, an open loop for the next video.
- **CTA:** soft and relational only. Thanks long-time viewers, welcomes new ones, teases the next video. No funnel push, unlike his 2025 framework videos.
- **Title formula:** Formula I in the playbook: How i made [year] my best year yet (hear me out...). Lowercase "i", superlative claim, a defensive curiosity parenthetical with an ellipsis that pre-empts skepticism.
- **Description:** Instagram handle only (the playbook's "personal" variant, no booking link).
- **Production:** unscripted, journal-derived, single take, ambient luxury setting.
- **Thumbnail:** not retrievable.

## Voice notes
- Ends statements with "right?" to pull the viewer along.
- Heavy intensifiers (genuinely, truly, insane, killer, absolutely crucial) and certainty markers ("I know for a fact").
- Profanity for emphasis, especially in the get-it-done passages.
- Conversational restarts, "um" fillers, self-correction, cheerfully losing count of his own list.
- Pre-emptive disclaimers ("not bragging", "not cruel").
- Mixes business, spiritual and lifestyle registers (Shiva and mala next to supercars and sales calls).
- Talks in USD and in monthly revenue tiers ($10k, $50k, $100k a month) as identity markers.
- Peer-to-peer, addressing both people ahead of him and beginners.
