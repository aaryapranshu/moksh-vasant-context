# Offers, pricing and sales: how Moksh thinks

Moksh builds an offer from the buyer's problems (each solution is "the flip" of a problem), prices it from the value of the problem, sells it through content, story sequences, DMs and a short call sequence, filters hard with price, and lets client results do the marketing.

Context for any agent loading only this file: Moksh Vasant (born 16 Dec 2003, Jamnagar; based in Bombay today [Aarya, 20 Oct 2026]) runs Meteoric Boost, a personal brand studio for founders. First clients signed 8 Jun 2025; "real selling" started in July 2025 (n1vbE3e8XuE [32:54 to 33:20]). He has built three client offers live on YouTube: Peak Form Protocol (Oct 2025), Dialed (Mar 2026) and "Buying a business in a box" (Apr 2026). Every revenue, price, close-rate and client figure is **self-reported and unaudited**; see [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md). Timestamps are `[mm:ss]` in the video named by its ID.

## Top 5 in one line each

1. **Price from the value of the problem:** about 5% of what the problem costs the buyer, or a share of the value created; never a price set by deliverables.
2. **Problems make the offer:** stack 10 to 20 real ICP problems; each solution is the flip; solving one creates the next (the upsell).
3. **The funnel is a conversation:** content, profile, story sequence, keyword DM, setter, short call, pre-sale asset, close.
4. **Price high and scale by price:** "Charge as high ticket as you can and that is the best filtering mechanism".
5. **Give the method away, charge for implementation and access.**

### 1. Price from the value of the problem

**The idea:** A price comes from what the buyer's problem costs, or what the solution produces, not from deliverables or gut feel. Work out the value, take a small slice, and make the return obvious.

**How he applies it:**
- Estimate what the problem costs a year, then charge about 5% of it (n1vbE3e8XuE [43:46]).
- B2B value math (Dialed): an owner making $20k a month works about 120 hours, so an hour is worth about $166, and 20 hours a month closing costs about $3,300 (qmN62Kp4ohg [17:48]). Add the revenue the solution produces, take about 5%, then collapse it into a rule: setup fee = the owner's own ticket size, plus a 5% share (qmN62Kp4ohg [1:06:10 to 1:11:32]).
- Training price anchored to half a trainee's first month of minimum commission: about $500 against about $1,000 (qmN62Kp4ohg [1:12:28 to 1:14:06]).
- Apr 2026: "a percentage of the value we're generating for them which is what we will calculate" (AnqK0H6Uhis [34:11]). No number on camera.
- Price tracks the client's delta: a Fortune 500 buyer gains more than a local business, so pays more (9Ma2bozEqGM [1:36:27]).
- Anti-rule: "If everyone's charging 3K ... let me charge 3K ... do you have a brain or are you a goldfish?" (qmN62Kp4ohg [1:05:49 to 1:06:01]).

**In his words:** "Do you have a brain or are you a goldfish?" (qmN62Kp4ohg [1:06:01])

### 3. Problems make the offer (and the next problem is the upsell)

**The idea:** Solve 10 to 20 important problems for a rich market and the offer is valuable. Each solution is the flip of a problem. Solving one problem creates the next one, which is the upsell. Problems you solve for yourself become offers.

**How he applies it:**
- "If you're solving 10 to 20 really important problems of your ICP, you have a very valuable offer. And if you're catering to a rich market, you're going to be making money"; "the value should always seem way more than the price" (wi6pyq5nXK8 [11:13], [13:38]).
- Solutions are "the flip" of problems (AnqK0H6Uhis [55:51 to 56:46]).
- "You solve one problem for your icp just to create another one", and the next problem is the upsell (AnqK0H6Uhis [55:18 to 55:51]).
- Problems and solutions double as content ([content and distribution](content-and-distribution.md), model 5).
- "What problems you solve for yourself later turn into offers that you sell to others" (9Ma2bozEqGM [2:03:31]).

**In his words:** "You solve one problem for your icp just to create another one." (AnqK0H6Uhis [55:32])

**Best sources:** wi6pyq5nXK8 [11:13 to 13:38] (21 Oct 2025); AnqK0H6Uhis [55:18 to 56:46] (8 Apr 2026); 9Ma2bozEqGM [2:03:31] (11 Aug 2026).

**Evolution / confidence:** High.

### 5. The funnel: content to DM to close

**The idea:** A conversation replaces the landing page. Each step moves a stranger closer to a call, and the sale is qualified and booked through DMs. Once you have inbound, you won't go back.

**How he applies it:**
- The profile funnel (Mar 2026): short-form reach; the profile as landing page; long-form YouTube with testimonials; a story sequence or warm outbound; a keyword DM; an appointment setter; a pre-sale asset; a one-call close; onboarding, quick win, testimonial, upsell (qmN62Kp4ohg [58:07]).

### 9. Price high and scale by price, not by clients

**The idea:** There are two ways to scale: more clients or higher prices. He picks price, because more clients means the team "will die in the fulfillment". A high price also selects committed clients, who get better results and attract more good clients.

**How he applies it:**
- "Charge as high ticket as you can and that is the best filtering mechanism to attract the best type of founders" (n1vbE3e8XuE [44:07 to 44:19]).
- The virtuous pricing cycle: high price, committed client, better results, more good clients (IEuERGHU3wA [25:15]).

- By Oct 2026 he says he charges about 3x competitors (NUp6EiXzojA [02:58]); fewer, higher-quality clients on retainers is the plan (9Ma2bozEqGM [2:09:13 to 2:09:27]).

### 12. The scripted close

**The idea:** The closing call follows a fixed order that gets emotional commitment before the price, then states the price casually and takes payment on the call.

**How he applies it:**
- Order (Jan 2026): rapport; agenda; confirm they watched the pre-sale asset; emotional pain questions; pre-handle objections (why now, cost of doing nothing, what they tried, is he the right person, have they invested before, do they decide alone); paint the dream; recap; ask what he missed; 0 to 10 readiness; state the price; handle objections with stories; take payment or a deposit (BU0smWywaw8 [29:31 to 32:10]).
- Reading the room: "if they're looking up in the sky, that means they're feeling something" [31:26]; pitch the price "as if it's no big deal" [31:59]; "I just get paid in full on the sales call itself" [32:10].
- His underlying check: they feel the pain, picture the dream, see the gap, and see him as the credible way across (n1vbE3e8XuE).
- What he teaches clients (via Satvik): follow-ups, deposits on calls, pre-sale (z_dxsfu9NZY [15:32 to 16:32]).

**In his words:** "Never lose your frame on a sales call." (n1vbE3e8XuE [42:11])

**Best sources:** BU0smWywaw8 [29:31 to 32:10] (13 Jan 2026); n1vbE3e8XuE [42:11] (Mar 2026).

**Evolution / confidence:** For roughly his first six weeks he couldn't close high ticket; he hired a sales coach in July 2025 and sent him call recordings (n1vbE3e8XuE [33:56]; the tool is "Fathom" in the re-edit, "Vilo" in the original captions). ### 13. Objections: find the real one, re-close with three yeses

**The idea:** The first objection is rarely the real one. Find it, stack yeses, hold frame, and don't leave without a decision.

**How he applies it:**
- "Let me think about it" is a mask (no money, no belief, or a competitor). Re-close with three yeses: am I the person, is now the time, do you not want to do it yourself (n1vbE3e8XuE [34:42 to 35:51]). The example is the host Sanskar Goyal's own sales call with Moksh, so the story is told to its subject.
- Toughest call: a Hong Kong SaaS founder wanted two weeks to travel. Moksh: those two weeks are when foundations, team and the first batch get built. Then back to the pain: two years of trying with gear and lights, no traction. Deposit now, balance once his invoice cleared. About two hours; signed (n1vbE3e8XuE [37:21 to 41:58]).
- Identity frame on an ego-driven prospect: act like the person you want to be (n1vbE3e8XuE [40:12]).
- Flex terms, not price (n1vbE3e8XuE [41:02]).

**In his words:** "I'm a big believer in acting like the person I know I want to be rather than acting like the person I am today." (n1vbE3e8XuE [40:12])

**Best sources:** n1vbE3e8XuE [34:42 to 41:58] (Mar 2026).

**Evolution / confidence:** Dialed's closer curriculum (trap questions, English for authority and Hindi for connection) may be partly Arjun's; the transcript has no speaker labels. High for the TSS material.

### 17. Founders should not close their own calls

**The idea:** Once demand exists, a founder on sales calls wastes the scarcest resource and lowers their authority. Owners at $10k to $20k a month need a closer.

- Closers need warm inbound, so lead gen drives everything.
- His own path: he closed his own deals in 2025; Arjun Sharin started closing in the last week of Jan 2026 ($46k in contracts in 30 days, about 25 calls and 12 sign-ups) (qmN62Kp4ohg [41:42 to 42:12], [1:06:53]). ### 18. The first month decides retention

**The idea:** "Retention is everything", and "the first month is everything". A first win kills buyer's remorse, so engagements start with momentum.

**How he applies it:**
- Quick win in month one: first cash collected or first deal closed (qmN62Kp4ohg [45:56 to 48:03]).
- Dopamine front-loading: a full roadmap within 24 hours because "Clients want action items" (wi6pyq5nXK8 [20:19 to 20:56]).
- He onboards new clients himself, the same day (qSNYWCyKGyo [40:18]); clients get his WhatsApp (w5ONkdV9OZw [23:26]).
- Perceived value wins the first sale; delivered value keeps the retainer (9Ma2bozEqGM [1:33:23]).

**In his words:** "Maximize the amount of dopamine a client gets within the first 24 hours." (wi6pyq5nXK8 [20:56]) / "Sales pay for dinner, results pay for retirement." (qmN62Kp4ohg [50:52], likely Moksh; no speaker labels)

**Best sources:** qmN62Kp4ohg [45:56 to 50:52] (22 Mar 2026); wi6pyq5nXK8 [20:19 to 20:56] (21 Oct 2025); 9Ma2bozEqGM [1:33:23] (11 Aug 2026).

**Evolution / confidence:** High.

### 19. Accountability both ways instead of a guarantee

**The idea:** No refund or performance guarantee. Both sides commit to their part, and the team holds itself to a higher internal bar than the client's goal.

**How he applies it:**
- Dialed: an internal benchmark above the client's goal; the team owns any shortfall if leads were good; the owner must supply good leads (qmN62Kp4ohg).
- Site: no guarantee of followers or revenue; if the founder does their part and goals aren't met, the studio keeps working at no cost.
- Client framing: "I believe in Moksh that he will be able to recoup it for you within 90 days" (Daksh, C4ckCV1xmj8 [15:10 to 15:29]); a client's words, not a promise.

**In his words:** "We just take the most pessimistic assumption." (qmN62Kp4ohg [32:44])

**Best sources:** qmN62Kp4ohg [32:44], [45:56 to 57:56] (22 Mar 2026); site copy (2026).

**Evolution / confidence:** The real terms sit in a private contract. High.
