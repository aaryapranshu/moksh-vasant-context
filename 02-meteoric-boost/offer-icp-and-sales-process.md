### Dream-client criteria (Aug 2026, London)
He says "eight things" and numbers seven (pyYGdcb1V48 [01:04 to 08:12]):
1. **A different industry.** Not someone "in my industry doing content for other people. They'll steal all of my frameworks".
2. **Doing better than me.** "My positioning online has always been to attract someone... who's ahead of me in the game".
3. **Real credibility.** "The track record is the actual authority"; "Manufacturing credibility never really works. It comes off as distasteful".
4. **Tech-inclined.** Open to new software.
5. **Taste**, and help articulating it.
6. **Speed and feedback.** "We don't like overthinkers. We like action takers."
7. **"Someone I can jam with."**
"It's not like I'm choosing my clients, but it's more like I'm curating the right winning environment around me" [07:57]. Also: "the biggest like unfair advantage you have is the fact that you just purely execute"; perfectionist clients are "the biggest pain" (AnqK0H6Uhis [1:08:32 to 1:08:47]).

### Who it's for (site, 2026)
- Earns $50K/month or more, or holds equivalent wealth
- Known in their industry but invisible outside it
- Wants recognition for work already done
- Wants authority, not just an audience
- Will give 4 to 8 hours a month on camera
- Is playing a five-year game

### Who it's not for (site)
- Anyone under $50K/month
- Price shoppers
- People who want a following more than a reputation
- People expecting results within 30 days
- Anyone who wants someone to just post on their behalf

### Red flags to listen for on a fit call (derived)
- Asks "how many followers will I get" before asking about reputation or deals.
- Wants results in a month, or wants the studio to post without appearing on camera.
- Negotiates on price before understanding the scope.
- Cannot commit 4 to 8 hours a month, or overthinks every step ("we don't like overthinkers").
- Below the floor with no equivalent wealth.
- Runs a content or personal-branding business (criterion 1).

## 3. Guarantee and risk reversal

- **Site FAQ:** no direct guarantee of followers or revenue. If the founder does their part and the goals in the plan are not met, the studio keeps working with them at no cost.
- **Terms of Use:** outcomes on the site are examples, not promises; engagements run under a separate written agreement.
- **Two conditions carry the weight:** "founder does their part" and "outcomes stated in the plan". The real scope sits in the private contract (inference).
- **Benchmark above the client's goal (Dialed build, Mar 2026):** no refund promise; the team sets an internal benchmark above the client's goal ($15K against the client's $10K) and owns the shortfall if the client held up their side (qmN62Kp4ohg).
- **Timelines:** "we just take the most pessimistic assumption" (qmN62Kp4ohg [32:44]).
- **Client-voiced payback:** "I believe in Moksh that he will be able to recoup it for you within 90 days... provided you do everything this guy tells you" (Daksh, C4ckCV1xmj8 [15:10 to 15:29]). A client's belief, used in Meteoric's own cold opens (w5ONkdV9OZw [00:00]); not a guarantee.
- **Belief framing:** "you're investing your belief into us ... we will go above and beyond" (Moksh, k57qG2-WnwI [04:44 to 04:56]).

### 4.2 The model behind the funnel
- **Funnel as meeting someone:** curiosity ("someone wearing sunglasses indoors"), trust ("pain point, value. Pain point, value"), then "let's go grab a coffee" (n1vbE3e8XuE [19:37 to 23:03]).
- **Instagram is a direct funnel:** "The beauty of Instagram is it's a direct funnel" (n1vbE3e8XuE [23:03]).
- **Marketing does most of the selling:** story-sequence buying motivation runs from 0% to 80% to 100% (paid); "every sequence gets the buyer just closer to that 80% point"; "sales just does like 20%" (AnqK0H6Uhis [1:21:57 to 1:22:29]).
- **Presold is the target state:** "people arrive pre-sold"; every client in the Bali room came to the call already knowing him, the company and the team (qSNYWCyKGyo [09:08 to 09:24]).
- **Objections are upstream failures:** "Any objection you hear on a sales call is a content failure... Fix it upstream." Arjun reports repeated objections and the team makes a content piece (qSNYWCyKGyo [29:05 to 29:31]).
- **Testimonials channel in the path:** long-form value videos, then "my testimonials YouTube channel" and a 20-minute testimonial, then the Calendly in the description (BU0smWywaw8 [18:52 to 19:04]).

## 5. Discovery call

Rules (BU0smWywaw8, Jan 2026, unless noted):
- Length: "should not be more than 15 minutes. I've said 15 to 30 minutes over here" [28:00]. (The 2026 Calendly lists 60 minutes.)
- Never discuss price; "if the prospect is hot, book the closing call on the discovery call" [28:12 to 28:24].
- Build rapport and listen for "pain, frustration, and desire" [28:24 to 28:36].
- Typical problems he hears: no content system; a successful business with no visibility; a five-figure info product that wants to scale [28:36 to 28:54].
- Qualify on BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) [28:54].
- Build authority: "This is what Freddy does on the calls" [29:06].
- Know the ICP deeply before any call: "their routines, how they think about work and delegate, biggest pains and fears, what they are most successful at and proud of"; "I put some pictures to the ICP as well" (n1vbE3e8XuE [44:43 to 44:53]).
- 2026 version: the "lead strategist" audit call (w5ONkdV9OZw [26:00]) or the site's short fit call.

## 7. Closing call

### 7.1 Script (BU0smWywaw8 [30:42 to 32:19], Jan 2026)
"You can take a screenshot of this if you want" [30:42].
1. **Rapport.**
2. **Set the agenda.**
3. **Ask whether they watched the video** and if they have questions.
4. **Dig for pain** with emotionally charged questions: why aren't you where you want to be, what is stopping you, how long has it been an issue, when did you realise it was a real problem [30:54].
5. **Clear obstacles before the price:** why now, what happens if nothing changes, what have you tried, am I the right person, have you invested in yourself before, "are you in this alone or do you need someone else's buying" (probably "buy-in") [31:06 to 31:14].
6. **Paint the dream state** and drop in client stories. "If they're looking up in the sky, that means they're feeling something" [31:26].
7. **Relay** their pains, goals, motivation and dream outcome back; ask "did I miss anything?" [31:38].
8. **Readiness scale:** "On a scale of 0 to 10, where zero is not keen at all and 10 is let's go now"; below nine, ask what is missing and get them to 10 before the price [31:38 to 31:50].
9. **Pitch:** "for $5,000, here's exactly what you're going to get", stated "as if it's no big deal" [31:59].
10. **Handle objections** with stories, quotes, metaphors.
11. **Take payment on the call**, or a deposit plus a booked follow-up. "I just get paid in full on the sales call itself" [32:10].

"It took me 6 months to get to this point where people hop on calls and they have zero objections" (BU0smWywaw8 [05:03]).

### 7.2 What his sales coach drilled in (TSS, Mar 2026)
He sent call recordings to a coach for "very specific feedback on how to close, how to have more authority on calls, what frameworks to follow" (n1vbE3e8XuE [34:09]). On every call, check that the prospect feels the pain of where he is now, can visualise the dream outcome, sees the gap, and sees you "as the most credible source or the solution" [34:21]. Before the coach: "I was fumbling leads left, right and center" [33:56]; sales was a "skill issue" for the first month and a half (IEuERGHU3wA [20:31]).

### 7.3 The re-close (TSS, Mar 2026)
On Sanskar Goyal's own sales call: "I think I played to your ego the entire call." He reframed (you have the problem, want a credible solution, can't do it yourself), then re-closed with three questions: am I the person to help, is now the time, do you not want to do this yourself? Three yeses, then "why not onboard right now?" "Once you've gotten multiple yeses and you've kind of handled every objection ... they have no option but to close" (n1vbE3e8XuE [35:16 to 36:04]).

### 7.4 Rules he states
- "Never lose your frame on a sales call"; "emotionally just connect with the person"; "you don't leave the call till you close" (n1vbE3e8XuE [42:11]).
- "I'm a big believer in acting like the person I know I want to be rather than acting like the person I am today", used as a frame on an ego-driven prospect (n1vbE3e8XuE [40:12]).
- The call is "a value exchange", not "making money on that call" (wi6pyq5nXK8 [48:20]).
- Founders should not close their own calls; owners doing $10K to $20K a month need a closer, and the founder should become "a public personality" (Dialed build, qmN62Kp4ohg [43:35 to 44:22]). Arjun's own version: "You're the founder. You should be spoken to once action has been taken" and "on closing calls you don't sell information that much you sell feelings" (Arjun, qmN62Kp4ohg [16:24], [16:55]).
- "Sales pay for dinner, results pay for retirement" (likely Moksh, qmN62Kp4ohg [50:52]).
- Record every call (Fathom) and review it; he reviews transcripts with Claude (n1vbE3e8XuE [34:09]; AnqK0H6Uhis [1:06:33]).
- What he teaches clients: follow-ups, deposits on sales calls, pre-sale (Satvik, z_dxsfu9NZY [15:32 to 16:32]).
- What triggers him: a prospect saying "I don't know if this is worth it" when the money "is gonna be like 100x in the next few years" (qSNYWCyKGyo [08:29]).

### 9.5 Value math worked live (Dialed, Mar 2026)
1. Value the buyer's time: a $20K/month owner working about 120 hours is worth about $166 an hour, so 20 hours of closing a month costs about $3.3K (qmN62Kp4ohg [17:48]).
2. Add the revenue the solution produces; take about 5%.
3. Collapse it into a rule: the B2B setup fee equals the owner's own ticket size, plus a 5% share (qmN62Kp4ohg [1:06:10 to 1:11:32]).
4. Training program: anchor to half a trainee's first month of minimum commission ($1,000 at 10% of a $10K-a-month offer, so about $500) [1:12:28 to 1:14:06].
5. Anti-rule: "If everyone's charging 3K ... let me charge 3K ... do you have a brain or are you a goldfish?" [1:05:49 to 1:06:01].
6. Raise prices at a 50% close rate (Arjun crediting Moksh) [59:48].

## 12. Sales-call prep checklist (derived for agents)

A synthesis for preparing a Meteoric fit or closing call. Not a published company document.

1. **Pull the lead:** application fields, Instagram handle, how they arrived (DM keyword, site, podcast, ad, referral), which content they watched.
2. **Check fit:** the site floor ($50K/month or equivalent wealth); the current band (high six to nine figures a year); the seven dream-client criteria (section 2). Flag anyone in the personal-branding business.
3. **Audit their profile** with the 3-second questions (who, what, for whom, is it for someone like me) and note the cheapest fix (usually the bio).
4. **Find where their authority already lives** (LinkedIn, an industry, a portfolio) and the authority-transfer angle.
5. **Estimate the cost of the problem** (lost deals, launches from zero, weaker competitors getting the rooms) for value-based framing.
6. **Pick two or three matching cases** by archetype from [case-studies.md](case-studies.md), using the client's own figures and the "How to use client proof" rules.
7. **Prepare pain questions and the pre-handled objections** (7.1 step 5) plus the likely objections in section 8.
8. **Know the guarantee wording** exactly as the site states it; never promise follower counts, revenue or 30-day results.
9. **Decide terms in advance:** deposit plus later invoice is the pattern; discounting is not.
10. **After the call:** log the outcome; if not closed, send the pre-sale asset within 48 to 72 hours; review the recording; report any repeated objection so it can be fixed in content.
