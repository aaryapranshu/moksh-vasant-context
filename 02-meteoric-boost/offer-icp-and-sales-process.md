# Offer, ICP and sales process

What Meteoric Boost sells, to whom, at what price, and how a lead moves from a piece of content to a signed client, including the call scripts, objection handling and pricing logic Moksh describes; ends with a sales-call prep checklist.

Context for a cold reader: Meteoric Boost is Moksh Vasant's personal brand studio for founders (started 2025). All prices, close rates, margins and client numbers below are self-reported by Moksh, his team or the company site.

### Done-with-you vs done-for-you: how Moksh's framing changed
- **2025 to early 2026:** Moksh argued done-with-you info coaching beats a done-for-you agency. Agencies get paid per deliverable and run a fulfillment treadmill; info coaching sells a transformation from point A to point B, with higher margins. He criticized the typical Indian path of freelancer then done-for-you agency, and used a fitness-trainer analogy: a weak trainer hands over a plan, a great one builds an identity. Clients become a "2.0" version of themselves (BU0smWywaw8, 9SBwERbSze4).
- **2026 site:** sells a done-for-you partnership. "We build the brand. You build the business." The founder does no homework.
- **Description tagline (2026):** give all the value away for free and charge for the implementation.
- Reading this together (inference): the transformation and identity framing stayed, the delivery moved from teaching the founder to running the system for them, and the buyer moved upmarket. Do not describe the current offer as coaching or a course.

### Two client types (TSS, Mar 2026)
1. Founders with a back-end offer (course, high-ticket coaching, agency): Meteoric scales front end and back end.
2. Founders who want a brand only: Meteoric sets up a team and a content system.

## 2. ICP and anti-ICP

### Who it's for (site, 2026)
- Earns $50K/month or more, or holds equivalent wealth
- Known in their industry but invisible outside it
- Wants recognition for work already done
- Wants authority, not just an audience
- Will give 4 to 8 hours a month on camera
- Is playing a five-year game

### Who it's not for (site)
- Anyone under $50K/month
- Price shoppers
- People who want a following more than a reputation
- People expecting results within 30 days
- Anyone who wants someone to just post on their behalf

### Red flags to listen for on a fit call (derived from the anti-ICP)
- Asks "how many followers will I get" before asking about reputation or deals.
- Wants results in a month, or wants the studio to post for them without appearing on camera.
- Negotiates on price before understanding the scope.
- Cannot commit 4 to 8 hours a month.
- Business is below the floor and has no equivalent wealth.

## 3. Guarantee and risk reversal

- **Site FAQ:** no direct guarantee of followers or revenue. If the founder does their part and the goals in the plan are not met, the studio keeps working with them at no cost.
- **Terms of Use:** outcomes on the site are examples, not promises; engagements run under a separate written agreement.
- **Two conditions carry the weight:** "founder does their part" and "outcomes stated in the plan". The real scope sits in the private contract (inference).
- **Moksh's guarantee philosophy (Dialed build, Mar 2026):** no refund or performance guarantee; mutual accountability instead. The agency sets an internal benchmark above the client's goal (example: $15k against the client's $10k) and owns any shortfall if the client held up their side.
- **Timeline promises:** always quote the most conservative time to result (qmN62Kp4ohg).

- Oct 2025 version of the funnel: Instagram story sequence, DM keyword, appointment setter, discovery call, closing call. Sales metrics he tracked: DMs, calls booked, pre-sale assets sent, closing calls, cash collected (IEuERGHU3wA, self-reported).
- Early close-rate problem: for roughly the first six weeks he could not close high-ticket deals and called it a skill gap. He hired a sales mentor who reviewed every call, and says his close rate rose sharply after (no figures given) (IEuERGHU3wA).
- "DM is the only funnel" (BU0smWywaw8, Jan 2026): no website, no VSL at that time. By 2026 a long-form sales site with an apply form exists, so this rule changed.
- Booking moved from Moksh's own Calendly (2025) to Arjun's "Meteoric 1:1" 60-minute Calendly (about Feb 2026 onward), described as a call to assess goals, commitment and the current situation.
- Presold inbound is the target state: a Sep 2026 client (an AI-infrastructure founder at $70k+ MRR) watched part of a podcast with another client, came in presold, and was onboarded within 48 hours (M8TkhDOCZlE).

### Site application flow (Oct 2026)
1. Apply button on the site.
2. Five fields: name, email, phone with country code, business or role, Instagram handle (only the handle is optional).
3. Every application is read and answered by a person; no automated decisions. Stored in Supabase; reviewed in a private Google Sheet (moved from Notion on 16 Sep 2026); kept 24 months.
4. A short fit call with a strategist, who will say directly if it is not a fit. The site says the studio would rather lose a deal than build the wrong brand.
5. A Meta pixel fires on page view and on a completed application (inference: paid traffic is being measured).

### Gated CTAs (how leads are filtered before they book)
- Book only if you are a serious founder or operator who can invest; everyone else keeps watching the free content (H68gyWOlIy4, BU0smWywaw8, Ze4JOAAvAq8).
- Ration CTAs and use scarcity (limited spots, limited free calls).

## 5. Discovery call (Jan 2026 rules)
- About 15 minutes; he warns against going longer.
- No price talk.
- Qualify with BANT: budget, authority, need, timing.
- Purpose: build authority and decide whether to send the pre-sale asset.
- Know the ICP deeply before calls: daily routine, delegation habits, pains, fears, achievements, what they are proud of. Moksh builds avatar pictures so he can speak to them directly (n1vbE3e8XuE).

## 6. Closing call script (BU0smWywaw8, Jan 2026)

Order as Moksh described it:
1. **Rapport.**
2. **Agenda** for the call.
3. **Confirm they watched the pre-sale video**; take questions.
4. **Emotional pain questions:** why they aren't there yet, what's stopping them, how long it has been, when they realized it.
5. **Pre-handle objections before price:** why now, cost of doing nothing, what they've tried, is Moksh the right person, have they invested in themselves before, do they decide alone.
6. **Paint the dream state.**
7. **Recap** pains, goals and motivation back to them.
8. **Ask what he missed.**
9. **Readiness scale:** ask for a 0 to 10 rating and probe until it reaches 10.
10. **Pitch the price casually**, alongside what's included.
11. **Handle objections** with stories, quotes and metaphors.
12. **Take payment on the call** (preferred) or a deposit plus a booked follow-up.

What a sales coach drilled into him (TSS, Mar 2026): build authority on the call, follow a framework, and check four things: the prospect feels the pain, pictures the dream outcome, sees the gap, and sees Moksh as the credible solution.

## 7. Objection handling

| Objection | Moksh's approach | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to think about it" | Treat it as a mask for no money, no belief in you, or a competitor. Find the real objection, dig deeper, stack several yeses, then ask for the close. He walked through a call where he got three yeses from a "let me think" prospect | n1vbE3e8XuE |
| "I want to start in two weeks, I'm travelling" | Reframe the delay: those two weeks are when foundations, context, team setup and the first content batch get built, so content is ready when they land | n1vbE3e8XuE |
| "I'd rather do the creative myself" | Point to the pain: two years of trying, expensive gear and a lit studio, no traction. Then appeal to identity and ego by describing a founder who already became a thought leader | n1vbE3e8XuE |
| Cash-flow timing | Flex on terms, not price: deposit upfront, invoice the rest later | n1vbE3e8XuE |
| "Is this the right time / the right person?" | Pre-handled before price (step 5 of the script) | BU0smWywaw8 |
| Price | Price is never discussed on discovery; on the close it comes after a 10/10 readiness score. High price is framed as a filter for better clients | BU0smWywaw8, n1vbE3e8XuE |

The toughest call he describes: a Hong Kong-based SaaS founder with sales experience, about two hours, three objections in sequence (timing, DIY, cash flow). The prospect signed. His takeaways: never lose frame under pressure, connect emotionally, don't leave the call until it closes (n1vbE3e8XuE).

Other selling beliefs he states:
- Founders should not close their own calls: it lowers their authority and takes time from content and lead generation (qmN62Kp4ohg).
- Closing is selling the feeling and the pain, not the information; help prospects get out of their own way and do not avoid confrontation (qmN62Kp4ohg).
- Ruthlessness on calls means pressing on pain points and quoting high prices, decisive but not cruel (nnMuox4BtbU).
- Record every call (Fathom) and get it reviewed (n1vbE3e8XuE).
- Use English to build authority and Hindi to build connection (said about closers on calls, qmN62Kp4ohg).

## 8. Pricing logic

### Value-based pricing (TSS, Mar 2026)
- Estimate what the problem costs the client (his example: about ₹1 crore of lost leads in a year).
- Charge a small slice, about 5% of that cost.
- Never price on deliverables.
- Raise prices as results and close rate improve.
- A high price filters for better clients.

### Value math, worked live (Dialed build for Arjun, Mar 2026)
1. Put a dollar value on the buyer's time: a $20k/month owner working about 120 hours is worth about $166 an hour, so 20 hours of closing a month costs about $3.3k.
2. Add the revenue the solution produces.
3. Take a small share (5%).
4. Collapse it into a rule the client can repeat. Moksh's stated conclusion: the B2B setup fee equals the owner's own offer ticket size.
- The live arithmetic did not add up as spoken. Use the method, not the numbers.
- For a training program: anchor the price to the buyer's first month of earnings so it pays for itself in under a month.
- Call it an investment, not a fee. Revenue share keeps incentives aligned. Cap capacity (10 spots) and raise prices as demand grows.

### Pricing an offer built for a client (fitness coach, Nov 2025)
About $1,500 for four months, rising as results build; perceived value must exceed price, so stack 13 to 16 pain points each paired with a fix (wi6pyq5nXK8). This is the client's price, not Meteoric's.

## 9. Margins, CAC and unit economics (self-reported)
- Net margin about 79%, costs about 21% (software plus team), no inventory or capex (BU0smWywaw8, Jan 2026).
- CAC about zero, all inbound, ads planned (Oct 2025, IEuERGHU3wA); about $50 to $100 per client, organic, no ads (Jan 2026).
- Price follows the client's gain: perceived value wins the first sale and delivery wins retention, so an enterprise pays more than a local business for similar work (9Ma2bozEqGM, Aug 2026). In Oct 2025 he described the same loop as a virtuous cycle: a high price brings a committed client, better results, then more good clients (IEuERGHU3wA).
- Close rate: Arjun's Jan to Feb 2026 run of about 12 sign-ups from 25 calls is about 48% (inference from his figures).
- Story sequences: about $3k to $35k each (Jan 2026).
- Ads: none through early 2026, planned later as an amplifier; by Sep 2026 Moksh says he is learning paid media.
- Analyst math, not his claim: at a $5k to $6k average, $50k a month needs roughly 8 to 10 closes.

## 11. Sales-call prep checklist (derived for agents)

A synthesis for preparing a Meteoric fit or closing call. Not a published company document.

1. **Pull the lead:** application fields, Instagram handle, how they arrived (DM keyword, site, podcast, referral), which content they watched.
2. **Check fit against the ICP:** $50K/month or equivalent wealth; known in their industry, invisible outside it; wants reputation over followers; can give 4 to 8 hours a month; five-year horizon.
3. **Audit their profile** with the four questions (who, what, for whom, for me?) and note the cheapest fix.
4. **Find where their authority already lives** (LinkedIn, an industry, a portfolio) and the authority-transfer angle.
5. **Estimate the cost of the problem** (lost deals, launches from zero, weaker competitors getting the invites) for value-based pricing.
6. **Pick two or three matching case studies** by archetype from [case-studies.md](case-studies.md), flagged as self-reported.
7. **Prepare pain questions and pre-handled objections** (section 6), plus the likely objections from section 7.
8. **Know the guarantee wording** exactly as the site states it; do not promise follower counts, revenue or 30-day results.
9. **Decide terms flexibility in advance:** deposit plus later invoice is acceptable; discounting is not the pattern.
10. **After the call:** log the outcome, send the pre-sale asset within 48 to 72 hours if not closed, and record the call for review.
