# The Meteoric Engine and the delivery system

How Meteoric Boost delivers a founder's personal brand: the published 5-stage, 12-week Meteoric Engine from the site, its operating principles, and the detailed delivery methods Moksh describes in his videos and podcasts (extraction, archetypes, one-of-one positioning, onboarding, persona, formats, filming, editors, batching, story sequences, analytics and sprints).

Context for a cold reader: Meteoric Boost is Moksh Vasant's personal brand studio for founders earning $50K/month or more. The 2026 offer is a 3-month done-for-you partnership where the founder spends 4 to 8 hours a month on camera and the studio runs everything else. Results figures quoted here are self-reported.

## 1. The Engine as published (site, Oct 2026)

Headline figures: **14 phases, 5 stages, 12 weeks, 4 owners.** The site says two phases are internal and never shown. Only the five stages are public. The names of the 14 phases were not found anywhere.

| # | Stage | Weeks | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Extract | 1 to 2 | Pull out everything true about the founder before building anything |
| 02 | Build Your World | 2 to 4 | Positioning, formats, visuals, wardrobe, and the specific audience |
| 03 | Show Up | 3 to 12 | The founder's only job: arrive dressed as directed, at the chosen location, with scripts ready |
| 04 | Production | 3 to 12 | Filmed, cut, quality-checked, posted |
| 05 | Compound | 6 to 12 | Weekly review, sharpen, repeat |

- Stages overlap on purpose. Content goes live from about week 3 while positioning is still being finished. Review loops start in week 6.
- FAQ version of the same flow: onboarding, then a brand strategy session, then the content system build, then a loop of recording and posting with weekly reviews.
- Results timing per the FAQ: content is live in the first weeks; distribution and inbound demand build across the three months.

### Deliverables listed on the site

| Component | What's included |
|---|---|
| Brand Positioning | 1 locked roadmap |
| Content Strategy | 3 formats per founder |
| Founder Positioning | 1 one-of-one angle |
| Creative Direction | 1 deep session |
| Distribution | 7 sequence types |
| Content Production | Weekly, in batches |
| Editing | Every asset reviewed |
| Analytics and Iteration | Reviewed weekly |

### Operating rules (site)
1. If the team can do a task, the founder never touches it.
2. If a document can be built from a transcript, it is never a form.
3. If a decision can be made internally, the founder does not sit in a meeting for it.
4. Every phase has a named owner and a fixed week.

### Principles (site, "Why Meteoric")
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| It's not homework | The studio builds and runs the engine. No education, worksheets or deliverables handed back to the founder |
| Extraction, not invention | A brand built as a costume shows. Work starts from what is already true about the founder |
| The room you're never in | Three principals work on the brand without the founder on the call. The founder gets the conclusion, not the meeting |
| 4 to 8 hours a month | Camera time is the whole ask. If it takes more, the system was built wrong |
| Taste is the deliverable | The standard of work is visible before anyone pays |
| One of one | No templates. Each founder gets a single angle nobody else holds |

Moksh says he personally runs the creative vision layer on every client brand (site founder note).

## 3. Where the method came from (Moksh's own system)

Before Meteoric Boost, Moksh ran his own brand the same way (Epiphanies #12, #13, #15, Apr to May 2025, self-reported):
- Journals nightly and weekly; pulls 5 to 6 core topics from recurring themes.
- Turns brain dumps into hook, body and outro scripts.
- Batch-records 6 to 7 voiceovers, adds music.
- Hands editing and distribution to a team working in Notion; takes one review call a week.
- About 2 to 3 hours a month for short-form, about 24 hours a month all in.

Inferred mapping: journaling became Extract, batch recording became Show Up, the Notion team became Production, the weekly call became Compound. The site's 4 to 8 hours is his own few hours plus filming on location.

## 4. Stage 1, Extract: getting what is true

### Context gathering (TSS, Mar 2026)
Every brand starts with context: backstory, ideal viewer, ideal customer, ambitions, unfair advantages, inspirations and the on-camera persona. That context feeds the brand formula and later the scripting workflow.

### Extract Your Edge (H68gyWOlIy4, Aug 2025; qYIKwqRvIyU, Sep 2025)
1. **Ambitions.** Write 5 to 10 year goals for the person and the brand (impact, money, travel, creative fulfilment). A brand is an infinite game, played for at least a decade.
2. **Ideal Viewer Profile (IVP).** Who watches. His own: ambitious, young, smart Indians who relate to his life.
3. **Ideal Customer Profile (ICP).** Who buys. His own: founders and wealth creators (legacy family businesses, B2B AI automation firms, high-ticket sellers).
4. **Ideal Investor Profile.** Added in Aug 2025: design the brand to attract capital too.
5. **Attractive character.** Borrowed from Russell Brunson's four archetypes: leader, adventurer, reporter, reluctant hero. Pick two and hold them for about 18 months. Moksh's pair: adventurer (documenting the journey, drives reach) and leader (teaching the ICP, drives authority and sales). Examples he gives: Chris Williamson and Jay Shetty as reporters; a lead-gen founder who started posting late as a reluctant hero.
6. **Backstory and parables.** Write an unfiltered 1,500 to 2,000 word autobiography (a voice note transcribed with AI works). Pull 5 or 6 parables: pivotal moments that flipped a switch. Weave them into scripts for emotion.
7. **Unfair advantages.** Name them without apology: accent, looks, fitness, intelligence, taste, an upbringing that allowed failure. Moksh lists his own as an English-sounding accent, his looks, fitness and an eye for aesthetics.
8. **Inspiration from outside the niche.** Never name other creators as inspiration (copying puts you in their bucket). Use music, films, fiction and books (James Bond and Harvey Specter are his examples). Be a "purple cow" (Seth Godin's term).

### India note
Moksh calls India a trust-driven economy: a broad audience of 100K+ creates authority that carries into sales calls, so niche purity matters less there (qYIKwqRvIyU).

### Onboarding form (sD4R8Z4H6U4, Mar 2026)
- Long by design. It makes the client reflect on goals and on what they are willing to share, and it productizes their expertise.
- The site's rule (documents built from transcripts, never forms) is the 2026 position. The case-study client described a form. Read this as an evolution or a mix: a short intake plus recorded sessions that the team turns into documents (inference). Confirm with Moksh which one is current.

### Qualification on the first call
Moksh judges whether a profile is "easy to scale". The ideal profile already has real authority, revenue and an interesting life and only lacks distribution and status (sD4R8Z4H6U4).

## 5. Stage 2, Build Your World

### One-of-one brand formula (qYIKwqRvIyU; H68gyWOlIy4)
- **Life buckets or infinite games.** 3 or 4 life pursuits; business is always one. Moksh's: personal branding, online business, lifestyle (meditation, fitness, mindset).
- **Signature format as IP.** Engineer a format that is yours. Moksh cites 75+ (Aug 2025) and about 90 (Mar 2026) accounts copying his as proof it works.
- **Visual identity.** A distinct look, set with a Pinterest mood board.
- **Cadence.** Low frequency, high quality (see the cadence conflict in section 10).

### Persona design
- Client picks reference profiles plus fictional or movie characters to pin down the persona and visual identity (sD4R8Z4H6U4).
- Personal branding is narrative control, shaping how people see you before they meet you (sD4R8Z4H6U4).

### Authority transfer
Find where the founder's credibility already lives (for one client, LinkedIn, where about 95% of his business came from) and carry it to a new platform with a different flavor (lifestyle on Instagram). Do not cross-post or repackage the old content (sD4R8Z4H6U4).

### Goal-matched builds
No single playbook. Some clients want mass reach; others want a narrow following of the right people. Some never aim for virality and use content only to build trust before outbound (Ze4JOAAvAq8, n1vbE3e8XuE).

### Two client types (TSS, Mar 2026)
1. Founders with a back-end offer (course, high-ticket coaching, agency): Meteoric scales both the front end (brand) and the back end (offer, funnel).
2. Founders who want a brand only: Meteoric sets up a team and content system.

### Content pillars (sD4R8Z4H6U4)
- Storytelling first (Moksh's claimed edge).
- Lead magnets second: broad, top-of-funnel, cutting-edge topics in the client's field.
- Content is tailored: technical for an AI founder, lifestyle or art for others (n1vbE3e8XuE).

### Substance checklist (Ze4JOAAvAq8, Sep 2026)
Content fails for lack of substance, not format. Six things to put on camera:
1. Range: the real emotional arc of an operator's day, from morning conviction to evening doubt.
2. Current moves and fears: the two or three live bets and the tension around them.
3. Authority with taste: past work, clients and testimonials, documented calmly, never gloated.
4. Wins and momentum: new offices, clients, marketing moves that worked. Meteoric travels to clients' cities to film testimonials.
5. Audience quality: judge posts by who engages, not how many.
6. Contrarian takes: say what gives you the ick about your industry.

### Profile as landing page (0jNq7A4_yOM, Sep 2026; BU0smWywaw8; qmN62Kp4ohg)
A cold visitor must decode the profile in about three seconds. Four questions in order: who you are, what you do, who it's for, is it for someone like me.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Picture | Tight, well lit, face clearly visible. Refresh a few times a year |
| Bio | Role and company, who you help, how, and with what results, plus a DM keyword CTA |
| Bio link | A pre-sell video (a long video on how he built his brand, or a VSL-style asset) |
| Highlights | Each answers one hesitation: Start Here (10 to 15 stories), life, thoughts or mental models, ideal-client reel, client proof, Q&A, team |
| Pinned posts | Three: a client testimonial edit, a team-culture video, a personal intro (video preferred). Earlier version: two pinned carousels, one for recognition seekers and one for info-product owners who want to monetize |
| Grid | One visual theme per client (Moksh's own is motion: travel, cars, suits) so the aesthetic previews the work |

Fixing the bio is the first and cheapest move in any audit (qmN62Kp4ohg).

### High-status aesthetics (HPCsujvgIvE, ~Apr 2026)
Scripts and tools are commodities; taste is the moat.
- Principles: less is more; make effort look effortless (sprezzatura); share struggles only when they are later resolved on camera; show life in motion instead of posing; treat life as a documentary; flex taste, not money; coordinate colors; curate the whole frame; post for one specific person.
- Mood board of the life you want, built before you can afford it. The brand is a starter pack of objects and vibes.
- Shot library: side angles, front gaze, follow-from-behind, sunlit hero shots, POV lifestyle, fitness as a hobby, focused work.
- Color grade: an old-money-meets-young-rich target, natural grades with no visible filter. Palettes matched to the video's emotion (dark industrial, warm desert, tropical sage or teal, universal blue). Test: viewers should think the person looks good, not notice the grade.
- B-roll categories (n1vbE3e8XuE): work (desk from back, top or side; calls and meetings), screens, lifestyle in motion (driving, dinner, walking), objects (shoes, steering wheel, textures), workspace POV, fitness. Documenting life signals the person is always busy and worth following. Grades shift with season and place (warmer and darker in winter, bright and ocean-blue in the tropics).

## 6. Stage 3, Show Up: the founder's only job

- The founder arrives dressed as directed, at the chosen location, with scripts ready (site).
- **Batching.** For his own brand: about 2 days a month, one for filming and one for scripting and voiceovers, for about 6 to 8 videos (qYIKwqRvIyU).
- **Filming support ladder.** Remote camera setup over FaceTime first, then a local crew in the client's city for batch filming (sD4R8Z4H6U4).
- **Formats used with clients.** Raw talking-head videos (often in the car), lifestyle set pieces (a car shoot in Beverly Hills), case-study and big-brand proof content (sD4R8Z4H6U4).
- **Hook prep on set** (n1vbE3e8XuE): good sunlight (dark footage loses), a good outfit with the face visible, the person's unfair advantage on show (a fitness coach leads with his physique).
- **Founder time conflicts** (keep): 4 to 8 hours a month (site); about 4 hours a month (Whop); 8 to 12 hours a month of content work (said in the Mar 2026 live build about Arjun's own brand); Moksh's own 2 to 3 hours for short-form (Apr 2025).

## 7. Stage 4, Production

### Scripting
- Moksh writes his own scripts because the style is his.
- For clients, the team built an internal AI workflow on Claude that uses the context gathered in the first weeks plus a custom prompt. Each client's voice is matched. Hard rule: scripts must never sound like AI (n1vbE3e8XuE).
- Two hook types for clients: a vivid specific scene, or a proven identity or age hook.
- Reel skeleton: Hook, Authority, Flow, CTA (qYIKwqRvIyU).

### Reel formula (TSS, Mar 2026)
A broad hook built on age plus a flex, a pattern-interrupt opening visual, a cut every 1 to 1.5 seconds, house music that builds, a flowing script, about 60 seconds, posted about 30 minutes before the audience peaks. If the first four or five clips land, the video works.

### Editors
| Topic | Practice | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Client editors | Team sources several editors; the client picks one, because the client's taste has to match the editor and the story aesthetic | sD4R8Z4H6U4 |
| First hire | A vetted editor from his pool, with SOPs | H68gyWOlIy4 |
| SOP | When he hired his first editor (around Feb, year unconfirmed) he wrote an SOP on how he thinks about edits | n1vbE3e8XuE |
| Hiring funnel | One story post brought about 120 applicants, about 30 qualified (Sep 2025); 67 applications for a long-form editor from a single story (Aug 2025). Application: a 60-second video on why they want the role plus a portfolio | qYIKwqRvIyU, H68gyWOlIy4 |
| Trials | Paid trials only; he refuses unpaid test projects because unpaid editors are not motivated | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| Rates (his own content, Sep 2025) | About $15 per video from editors in India, Pakistan or Bangladesh, about $60 a week for 4 videos | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| Role | A great editor acts like an art director who asks questions that improve the story | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| QC | Every asset reviewed (site) | Site |

### Posting
- His own audience: Friday to Sunday around 8pm IST because the audience is mostly Indian (qYIKwqRvIyU).
- General rule: post about 30 minutes before the audience's peak (n1vbE3e8XuE).

### Content formats and their jobs
Moksh's rule: every format has one job, and views only matter for reach formats.

| Format | Job | Source |
|---|---|---|
| B-roll plus voiceover plus house music | Reach. One such reel did about 3.2 to 3.4M views and about 45k followers | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| Whiteboard explainers / ICP value videos | Leads and trust with buyers; views do not matter | qYIKwqRvIyU, BU0smWywaw8 |
| Raw talking-to-camera documentation | Connection and relatability | qYIKwqRvIyU, BU0smWywaw8 |
| "Edits" (graded, music-synced aspirational clips) | Fun, usually Sundays | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| Long-form (YouTube, masterminds, workshops, case studies, 20 to 30 min testimonials) | Authority and nurture; proof placed inside | BU0smWywaw8, qmN62Kp4ohg |
| Carousels | Pattern interrupt, value, CTA. Pinned carousels explain the mechanism | qYIKwqRvIyU, BU0smWywaw8 |
| Infographics | Commissioned from an editor | qYIKwqRvIyU |
| Story sequences | Conversion (below) | All |

Advice: run 2 or 3 formats per brand, each mapped to reach, leads or connection. The site sells "3 formats per founder".

The Jan 2026 framing calls this four-format ecosystem the **Butterfly Effect** (short-form in three types: broad whiteboard reach, ICP value, raw documentation; long-form; carousels; story sequences). A second named offer, the "Meteoric Boost Protocol", is mentioned but never explained (BU0smWywaw8).

### Three-layer funnel (TSS)
Top: curiosity (viral reels). Middle: trust (name a specific problem, then give value). Bottom: conversion (story sequences, ads, retargeting, DMs). He likens it to meeting someone in real life: curiosity, then trust, then commitment.

### Story sequences
| Type | What it does |
|---|---|
| CTA-driven | DM a keyword to book a call |
| Curiosity-driven | Lists symptoms to build problem awareness (a sleep-coach example) |
| Credibility-driven | Client praise and wins. He says these earn the most |
| Results-driven | Revenue, metrics, client outcomes |
| Value (Jan 2026 list) | Teaching inside stories |

- Types can be stacked (results plus CTA). Every sequence ends with a CTA. Ration CTAs to create scarcity (limited free calls) (qYIKwqRvIyU, BU0smWywaw8).
- Example structure from the Nov 2025 live build: pattern interrupt, identity gap, dream outcome, case-study proof, scarcity (two slots), keyword CTA. Drafted with Claude from the ICP and offer documents (wi6pyq5nXK8).
- Self-reported yield: about $3k to $35k per sequence, one cited at about $15k; a client with about 2,500 followers closed $65k from one sequence (BU0smWywaw8, n1vbE3e8XuE).
- Tracked per sequence: replies, booked calls, closes, drop-off.
- The site sells "7 sequence types" under Distribution; the seven are not listed anywhere.

## 8. Stage 5, Compound: analytics and iteration

### Weekly review
A 30 to 45 minute call each week (Monday or Tuesday for his own brand) to forecast posts and review data (qYIKwqRvIyU, H68gyWOlIy4). The site promises weekly analytics review.

### Engagement rate (qYIKwqRvIyU, Sep 2025, from his own data)
Engagement rate = (likes + saves + shares + DMs + comments + follows from that viewer) divided by views, times 100.

| ER | What he says happens |
|---|---|
| Above 7.5% | Wide distribution |
| Above 9% | About 60% of viewers are new |
| Above 11% | About 90% of viewers are new |
| Above 14% | Viral |

- Only two things matter to the algorithm: engagement rate and value. Four value types: inspiration, education, problem-solving, entertainment.
- **Fractional exposure.** Platforms test on a small batch, then expand about 10x per validated stage (5, 50, 500, 5,000).
- **80/20.** Three of his videos brought about 18k, 25k and 45k followers, roughly 90k of his 137k.
- **Low-engagement diagnosis.** Check the hook, the lighting, the dynamics and the speed of clip switching.

### Audience-quality audit (Ze4JOAAvAq8)
- Buyers mostly watch silently; Moksh says clients almost never liked or commented before signing. Loud engagement often comes from teenagers.
- Go through who engaged: look for verified or influential accounts and private accounts whose bios show serious businesses.
- Call posts assets, not content.

### Bulk and cut
An analogy he credits to a peer, Saul: alternate reach phases and conversion phases. If traffic is fine but conversion is weak, add ICP reels (for example 2 a week). His own 60k to 100k+ jump (March to June 2025) came from back-to-back reach content (qYIKwqRvIyU).

### Sprints and pauses
- Clients: content bursts, then a pause of about a month to build systems, then the next sprint. Goals reset after milestones (10K, then 100K) (sD4R8Z4H6U4).
- Moksh's own: 40-day sprints followed by 10 to 20 days of travel and rest (nnMuox4BtbU); 14 to 21 day sprints and 90-day quarters (newsletter era).

### Scaling what works (n1vbE3e8XuE)
When something works, screenshot it, analyze why, form a hypothesis, re-test, then delegate once proven.

## 9. Monetization, retention and the client relationship

### Monetization layer
- Turnkey, productized offers; low-friction automations; case-study posts (sD4R8Z4H6U4). Moksh says the agency's focus moved to monetization and back-end systems because followers are cheap and can be bought.
- **DM setter hire for a client** (sD4R8Z4H6U4): 6 or 7 candidates from a job board, application materials and Loom videos, shortlist of 2, live interviews, hire about 5 days later. The setter covers Instagram, X and LinkedIn.
- Low-view model for service businesses (n1vbE3e8XuE): a short list of high-value followers, trust content, an optimized profile, a 20 to 30 minute pre-sell video, a setter sending 50 to 100 targeted DMs a day, and every conversion metric tracked (open, reply, DM-to-conversation, booked, show-up, close).
- Offer builds for clients follow the field-by-field method in [offer-icp-and-sales-process.md](offer-icp-and-sales-process.md).

### Retention (qmN62Kp4ohg, Mar 2026)
- The first month decides retention. Engineer a quick win early (first cash collected, first deal closed) to kill buyer's remorse.
- Stay hands-on and easy to reach, including quick WhatsApp calls. Arjun described his own onboarding as help with scripts, editing and a roadmap.
- Front-load momentum: a roadmap delivered within 24 hours of signing (fitness-offer build, wi6pyq5nXK8).

### Operations and speed (Breaking down businesses, IEuERGHU3wA, Oct 2025)
- Turnaround to a client's first post and first win cut from about 21 days to about 12 days (self-reported).
- Delivery workflows mapped in Miro with green, yellow and red status.
- Onboarding automated end to end: payment, contract, forms, client portal and welcome messages, on n8n and Make.com, built by a friend.
- Claude Projects hold the client context gathered in the first weeks and feed scripting.
- AI is used only where it removes mundane work, not out of fear of missing out.

### Client relationship
- **Luxury as being taken care of** (NUp6EiXzojA, Oct 2026). Moksh defines luxury as the feeling of being looked after and wants the whole client experience, from the first WhatsApp message to the final testimonial shoot, to feel that way. The team flies to clients' cities for testimonial shoots.
- Always-on communication, late-night and early-morning calls across time zones; client responsiveness is the main speed driver (sD4R8Z4H6U4).
- In-person layer: client retreats (Bali, May 2026), filming at client locations, the founder network (qSNYWCyKGyo, Ze4JOAAvAq8, qmN62Kp4ohg).
- Testimonials are captured as part of fulfillment and fed back into marketing (n1vbE3e8XuE). Format: long, uncut interviews (10 to 30 minutes) rather than quote cards.

## 11. Onboarding run sheet (derived for agents)

This is a synthesis of the sources above, not a published Meteoric document. Use it to prep, then confirm details with Moksh or the COO.

1. **Before kickoff:** read the application (name, business, Instagram handle), the fit-call notes and any recorded call. Check the profile against the four-question legibility test.
2. **Week 1 to 2, Extract:** run recorded context sessions; draft documents from transcripts: ambitions (5 to 10 years), IVP, ICP, ideal investor, two archetypes, a 1,500 to 2,000 word backstory with 5 or 6 parables, unfair advantages, out-of-niche inspirations, where existing authority lives.
3. **Week 2 to 4, Build Your World:** one-of-one angle; 3 or 4 life buckets; 2 or 3 formats each with a job; mood board, shot library, grade; profile rebuild (picture, bio, link, highlights, pins, grid); story-sequence plan; reach-vs-niche goal; offer review if there is a back end; editor shortlist for the client to pick.
4. **From week 3, Show Up and Production:** scripts from the Claude workflow in the client's voice; batch shoot days with wardrobe and location set; first assets live; quick win targeted in month 1.
5. **From week 6, Compound:** weekly review (ER against thresholds, audience-quality audit, sequence metrics); bulk-or-cut call; sprint or pause; capture a testimonial once results land.
