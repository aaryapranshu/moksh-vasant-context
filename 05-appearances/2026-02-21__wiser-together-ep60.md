# How he made $130,000 in 5 months at the age of 21 (Wiser Together EP.60)

Moksh's third Wiser Together appearance, recorded live in Dubai at the end of 2025: the full founding story of Meteoric (from the end of his Sydney internship to about 35 clients), the bet he made on himself, a burnout year, and a 2026 plan built on structure, health and inner work instead of a revenue target.

**Context for this file:** Moksh Vasant is the founder of Meteoric Boost (he calls it "Meteoric" here), a studio that helps founders and "wealth creators" build, scale and monetize personal brands. This is the most complete first-person account of how the company started. It is reflective, not tactical; for the tactical playbook see [The Sanskar Show #226](2026-03-21__sanskar-show-226.md).

**Revenue caveat:** the title's "$130,000 in 5 months" is the show's packaging. In the conversation Moksh only says he crossed six figures in revenue with about 35 clients in about 5.5 months (self-reported). Other sources give other figures ($52K in 3.5 months, about $40K spoken; $130K in 5 to 6 months; Rs 1.5 crore in 6 months; about $152K lifetime by Mar 2026). Do not reconcile them by guessing.

**Chapters:** 0:00 Intro · 1:14 A year of compound growth · 3:08 How Meteoric was born · 10:07 Betting everything on himself · 14:53 Burnout, recovery and what's next · 22:31 The book that changed everything · 31:20 Why action beats overthinking · 38:43 Nervous system, energy and abundance · 47:44 Richest vs happiest person in the world?

**Books in the show notes:** *Psycho-Cybernetics* (Maxwell Maltz), *The Inner Game of Tennis* (W. Timothy Gallwey), *$100M Offers* (Alex Hormozi).

## Key takeaways (Moksh's views only)
1. **External results lag internal growth.** He frames 2025 as about four to five years of compounding inner work that finally showed up as an explosion of outer results.
2. **Bet on yourself before you can afford to.** He bought an expensive offer-launch programme on instalments and tied the next instalment to signing three clients.
3. **No regrets.** The past built who he is, so regret has no value. He would change nothing.
4. **2025 was fight-or-flight; 2026 is structure.** Consistent sleep, a planned calendar, planned work, acting as a CEO rather than a founder, and his best physical shape.
5. **Momentum is hard to give up.** It can make you depend on chaos, which is a trap.
6. **Money is a crutch until it is solved.** Once it is, life shows you the next gaps: relationships, dating, character, health. He set no financial goal for 2026; the goal is who he becomes, with revenue as a by-product. (This conflicts with the "Road to $1 million in 2026" video posted on his channel the same day the episode went out, which names a $1M goal.)
7. **Health is the biggest lever,** because it sets how fully he can show up for himself and others.
8. ***Psycho-Cybernetics* made him a winner.** Self-image drives outcomes; you stay balanced only while moving toward a goal.
9. **Observe without judgment** (from *The Inner Game of Tennis*): look at mistakes with curiosity, do not overvalue highs or lows, keep a steady mental state. Calm acceptance of setbacks is the most powerful state for personal and financial success.
10. **Learn the route by moving.** Action teaches what planning cannot. He is learning to embrace uncertainty.
11. **Perception shapes effort.** The same run feels easy beside a steady partner and hard beside a struggling one. His summary line: "Everything is perception."
12. **His only paid mentor in 2026 is a mindset coach,** not a business or health coach.
13. **Happiest over richest,** though he sees the two as intertwined and treats financial security as the base for reflection. What you keep at the end is memories; his year's richness was its experiences.
14. **No universal truth, only your own.**

1. **Reunion [HOST].** Welcomes him back for a third time, recording in Dubai the day after a birthday dinner, and frames the episode as a reflection on the year. **[MOKSH]** says this show is the only podcast he has done so far. TSS #226 came later, but this conflicts with the Breaking down businesses interview recorded on 9 Oct 2025 and a Mar 2025 Journey Club recording mentioned in his newsletter (see the index).
2. **The year [HOST then MOKSH].** The host calls it a phenomenal year of inner and outer growth. Moksh calls it about five years in the making. The year itself was non-stop work and stress: hundreds of conversations, scaling the business, building a team, moving countries, travel, and a heartbreak (no details).
3. **What Meteoric is [MOKSH].** Helps founders and wealth creators build, scale and monetize personal brands. Started April 2025.
4. **Start of 2025 [MOKSH].** Final university semester in Sydney. Building a mentorship platform that connected Indian students going abroad with students at their dream universities (StuKonnect, named in other sources). Interning in private equity, often seven days a week. Trying to stay fit. From his boss, a seasoned ex-investment banker and operator, he learned process creation and documentation, work ethic and attention to detail.
5. **The platform's launch campaign [MOKSH].** He calls it one of the craziest startup pre-launch campaigns in India: five days of posts, about 1M views, about 3,500 Instagram followers and about 3,500 YouTube subscribers, and inbound from VC funds (all self-reported; the numbers are smaller in his Mar 2025 newsletter and grew with each retelling). Lesson: team-building, a clearly explained vision and the founders' unfair advantages gave him confidence he could build traction for others.
6. **April 2025, the turning point [MOKSH].**
   - His co-founder left for a job abroad and the venture ended.
   - The internship ended around 14 April (other sources say 17 to 22 April). At a farewell lunch his boss gave him detailed feedback.
   - Working for that boss: 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., sometimes to 2 a.m.; freedom to figure things out; budget and a company card for tools; a boss who sat with him until problems were solved.
   - He had built the boss an AI outbound voice agent that cold-called acquisition targets from a HubSpot list, and it worked. He stresses he is non-technical and does not code.
   - Options he weighed: an AI automation agency or a creative agency. He leaned to an agency for cash flow and the lifestyle he wanted, and his network of agency owners convinced him he could do better than them.
   - He had not applied for a single job since February.
   - The boss's advice: he offered a referral to a bigger PE fund but said Moksh would be a great analyst, not the best one. He should lean into his years of video and value-proposition content and move back to India, where momentum was building.
7. **Naming and launch [MOKSH].** The night before his last lecture he took a long walk (captions suggest the Darling Harbour area) and sketched the model. He tried domains like "Nirvana Studios" and "Blank Studios", found them generic, and picked "Meteoric", the name of his 2020 calisthenics coaching startup, because he felt emotionally tied to it. He was reading *$100M Offers* while shaping the offer.
8. **Grief and the bet [MOKSH].** His grandfather, whom he was very close to, died on 24 April 2025 (his newsletter says 25 April). On 28 April he had a sales call with a past mentor who launches and scales offers, and signed up for a large programme he could not afford, paid in instalments, with the next instalment tied to three signed clients. Routine through the Sydney winter: about nine hours of sleep, work the rest, gym three times a week, locked in a room.
9. **First clients and team [MOKSH].** First signing on 8 June 2025. Next was his best friend (captions: "Arian"), then an Instagram inbound. (One line in the transcript, where Moksh seems to tell the host he signed on as the first client, may mean the host was client number one; this is an inference, not confirmed.) He moved back to India. First hire: an ops manager, later COO, who had been on the student-platform team and reached out to him; they negotiated terms and that person learned on the job. He went from three clients to about 35 in about 5.5 months and crossed six figures in revenue (self-reported). Team: four people plus one intern. Brands changed and pivoted along the way. He says Dubai opened his horizons.
10. **Backstory of the friendship [HOST].** The host has watched Moksh's videos since Moksh was a young teen vlogger and assumes he faced judgment but kept going. They ran the Bondi half-marathon together with friends. Resting around km 17 or 18, Moksh talked about *Psycho-Cybernetics* and said with conviction that he would be the one to change things for his family, which moved the host to read the book. They had coffee a week before Moksh left Australia for good. The host notes Moksh's grandfather lived with the family in Jamnagar.
11. **Regrets [MOKSH].** None. Regret has no value.
12. **Burnout and 2026 [MOKSH].** 2025 ran on fight-or-flight: high stress showing physically. August to October was chaotic, with erratic sleep and little exercise or proper eating. He then spent about six weeks in Mumbai on recovery, sleep and routine. Momentum is hard to give up and can make you depend on chaos. He wants structure: consistent sleep, a calendar, planned work, CEO not founder. Physically he wants his best shape, and believes each past body transformation brought out a new version of him. **[HOST]** adds that discipline in mundane habits is the foundation for growth, and that living without parameters brings experience but little growth. **[MOKSH]** Health is the biggest factor; money as a crutch; no financial goal (see takeaways).
13. **Psycho-Cybernetics [MOKSH].** It rewired how he relates to goals; the Bondi conversation started it. He says the most intense family turmoil of his life came in December (likely 2024) and frames it as what happens right before change; his relationship with his parents is now the best it has been in a long time. His summary of the book: people are like bicycles and only stay balanced moving toward a goal; low mood often comes from not working toward the self-image you picture; live as your future self now; heal past failures ("mental bruises"); use visualization and meditation; know the failure traits (fear, anger, uncertainty, fixating on failure) and the success traits (confidence, self-belief). He recommends it to anyone.
14. **The Inner Game of Tennis [MOKSH].** Currently reading it. Retells the mirror story: a player with a bad backswing had failed under several coaches, but seeing himself in a mirror made him curious instead of angry. Lesson: observe without judgment; practise without praise or criticism. Applied example: he paid a hefty fine that morning (amount and reason not given) and felt neither upset nor elated, which he calls a steady mental frequency. **[HOST]** compares this to rockets and planes correcting course from feedback and says successful people treat setbacks as data. A Federer statistic on the low share of points even top players win comes up, with the exact figure hedged.
15. **Action vs overthinking [HOST leads].** The host argues the winner is whoever tries again, that very smart people talk themselves out of acting, that "delusion" is a superpower, and cites Hormozi (any goal is a number of tries away) and Bob Proctor (goals should stretch you beyond what you know how to do). **[MOKSH]** adds an analogy from his planned run around the Palm in Dubai the next day: you learn the route by moving through it, not by planning it. He is learning to embrace uncertainty.
16. **Nervous system and abundance [HOST leads].** The host talks about a regulated nervous system, growth from survival mode vs from rest, scarcity vs abundance, frequency and attraction, Naval on choosing your people and city, and breathwork and meditation. **[MOKSH]** admits the fight-or-flight year again, says he can sense scarcity in other people's energy, and mentions an unverified idea that a person's aura extends about 2 m. Then the running-partner analogy and his "Everything is perception" line.
17. **Mindset coach [MOKSH].** The only mentor he pays in 2026 (unnamed, cost not given). The process: diagnose and audit current thought patterns, then rewire them through meditation, breathwork, recovery and cold exposure. It takes time, at least 21 days by his account. **[HOST]** talks about "limitless" as the source of who you are, and conditioning vs reality.
18. **Richest vs happiest [MOKSH].** First says the two are intertwined, then picks happiest, because life is finite and wealth without happiness is pointless. Financial security is the base for spiritual reflection, and a comfort level of roughly "100K" may be enough (currency and period unclear). At the end, what is left is memories. **[HOST or unclear]** Happiness comes from not wanting; the richest are often miserable.
19. **Close.** **[HOST]** There is no single right answer; family and society project expectations onto you. **[MOKSH]** Agrees there is no universal truth, only your own, and calls the conversation eye-opening.
