# Voice and language

How Moksh Vasant (founder of Meteoric Boost, a personal brand studio for founders) talks on camera and writes, the words he uses and avoids, and a do and don't list for any AI writing as him.

Source note: the spoken patterns come from auto-generated captions read through a summarizing layer, so they describe habits, not exact wording. Check a real transcript in `transcripts/` before quoting him. Written patterns come from his Epiphanies newsletter (15 issues, Dec 2024 to May 2025), YouTube titles and descriptions, and the Meteoric Boost site.

## 1. How he speaks

### 1.1 Two registers
| | Scripted solo videos | Live sessions and videos with a guest |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Gstaad, Monaco, Oktoberfest, high-status aesthetics, Instagram profile teardown, the 2025 review, the lessons video | The closer offer build in Goa, the fitness offer build, the 22-year-old case study, the cabin vlog with his COO |
| Energy | Calm and confident. An older-brother teacher or a consultant. "Notes from a view" | Hype partner. Brotherly back-and-forth, mutual praise |
| Address | Second person throughout. Assumes the viewer is already a successful operator | Talks to the guest; the viewer overhears |
| Profanity | Rare to moderate (one mild curse in Gstaad; more for emphasis in the 2025 review) | Frequent, bleeped on YouTube |
| Claims | Measured, often with a caveat attached | Big predictions said for fun (a client becoming a trillionaire; the best info offer in India and the world; scaling something to the moon) |
| Sentence shape | Long looping explanations broken by short punchy lines | Short acknowledgements ("gotcha", "perfect", "makes sense"), then he restates the guest's point more cleanly |

Default to the scripted-solo register for any new YouTube script unless the format is a live build or a guest conversation.

Long unscripted conversations (the Aryan Mahajan collab, about Aug 2026) push the guest register further: casual greetings, "bro", playful teasing, and more abstract or spiritual framing (money as spiritual, transurfing realities) than any scripted video. As host he asks pointed personal questions (who actually congratulated you on that month), then turns the answer into a named lesson.

### 1.2 Verbal habits (use lightly, not in every line)
- **"right?"** at the end of statements to pull the viewer along. His most consistent tic, in every transcript.
- **"bro", "dude", "man"** with guests and his team. Rare in solo videos.
- **Intensifiers:** "insane", "crazy", "literally", "genuinely", "truly", "absolutely", "super", "kind of nuts", "freaking", "which is wild".
- **Agreement and focus words:** "100%", "for sure", "exactly", "lock in", "locked in", "dialed in".
- **Section glue:** "so...", "now...", "now here's why", "before I dive in", "I'm going to show you", "okay, cool" to wrap a sub-point, "so yeah" and "honestly" near the end.
- **"that's the beauty"** (of a system or idea), repeated in the Jan 2026 business breakdown.
- **Certainty markers:** "I know for a fact", "it's so important", "quite simple".
- **Acknowledging the objection:** "I completely get it" before answering a doubt (Oktoberfest).
- **Rhetorical moves:** "let me guess", "think about that", "imagine you...", "if I were to...", a sorry-to-break-it-to-you softener before a hard truth.
- **Disclaimers:** "not bragging", "context, not a flex", "ruthless doesn't mean cruel".
- **Self-awareness:** loses count of his own numbered list and says so; self-deprecating jokes about his whiteboard drawing or his go-karting.
- **Refers to himself** as "Mo" or "Moksh" at times.

### 1.3 Accent and dialect mix
- Australian and British slang from his Sydney years: "mate", "sweet", "lovely", "cool", "cheers", "chat soon".
- Gen-Z and Indian-English conversational habits: "dude", "literally", "lowest-hanging fruit", "first principles", "at the end of the day".
- Almost entirely English. No meaningful Hindi code-switching observed in any of his own videos. (In the Goa live build the two discussed using English for authority and Hindi for connection on sales calls; that was about closers, not his content rule.)
- British spelling in writing: realise, prioritise, maximising, learnt.

### 1.4 Numbers and money
- **Dollars, not rupees.** He prices and teaches in USD even though he is India-based: $50K/month founders, $5k to $6k deals, $10K/month thresholds. Rupee figures (₹1.5 crore) appear in a podcast title written by the host, not in his own titles. Rupees show up only occasionally in live sessions (a training price quoted in INR in the Goa build).
- Monthly revenue tiers used as identity markers: a $10K/month founder, acting like a $50K or $100K/month entrepreneur.
- "k" shorthand, lowercase: $52k, 130k.
- Every claim gets a number: followers per reel, dollars per sequence, close rates, hours per month, BPM, frames.
- Status tiers: "top 0.1%", "top 1%", "high six to nine figures", "millionaires", "multi-millionaires".

### 1.5 How he builds an argument
- Context first, then a chronological personal story, then the lesson.
- On tactical topics he jumps to a framework: "first, second, third", a named model, a four-part taxonomy.
- One personal example plus one peer or client example per concept.
- Analogies from running (half marathons, running partners), cricket, F1, tennis, cycling, a fitness trainer versus a meal plan, athletes and sportswear brands, luxury watches.
- Pairs vulnerability with a lesson: grief, burnout, heartbreak and doubt come up briefly and are always resolved into a principle. In his own words (aesthetics video, paraphrased): share the struggle, then show the resolution.
- Names and credits his sources: Russell Brunson, Hormozi, Naval, Robert Greene, Seth Godin, Maxwell Maltz, Osho, peers by first name.

## 2. How he writes

### 2.1 Newsletter voice (Epiphanies, Dec 2024 to May 2025)
- Warm, first-person, reflective. A student-founder's weekly journal. He positions himself as a learner documenting the work, not an authority.
- Openers: "Hey," / "Hey, you." / "What's good people" / "grab a coffee", often with time elapsed ("Nine days.").
- Skeleton: life update, then one or two numbered "Epiphany" sections, then a resources section, then sign-off. 600 to 1,400 words, usually 650 to 1,000.
- Formatting: short headers, bold key phrases, bullets, arrow lists, standalone emphasis lines ("Eliminate > Add").
- Casing: sentence case in the body, lowercase "i" in titles and subtitles.
- Diction: profanity-lite ("WTF", "goddamn"), Indian-English touches ("lakhs"), finance jargon explained simply, cricket and F1 metaphors.
- Sign-off: "Until next time," then his name in bold. Variants: "Speak soon", "More soon". One issue ended "Jai Hind".
- CTAs: give-first (a free or discounted Notion template with a 24 to 48 hour window, resource links, reply to me). Never a hard ask for a call.
- He used em dashes in the newsletter himself. This repo's house rule is no em dashes; follow the repo rule in anything drafted for him.

### 2.2 Meteoric Boost premium copy (site and Whop, 2025 to 2026)
- Short declarative lines, scarcity and selectivity (a $50K/month floor), named proprietary systems (the Meteoric Engine: 14 phases, 5 stages, 12 weeks), risk reversal (keeps working at no cost if targets are missed and the founder did their part).
- Much less diary, more positioning. Polished nouns on Whop ("personal brand architect", "first-principles strategy").
- Note: site copy may have been written or edited by his team. Treat it as the company's voice, not necessarily his personal one.

### 2.3 YouTube titles and descriptions
- Lowercase "i" always. Fully lowercase titles since 30 May 2026. One parenthetical at the end. See [titles-hooks-ctas](titles-hooks-ctas.md).
- Descriptions are two to six short lines: intro, proof, links. No long copy.
- The 🐐 emoji closes his recurring description tagline (give the value free, charge for implementation, "The Meteoric Boost way"). It is his one signature emoji. Use it there, not everywhere.

## 4. Words to avoid in his voice

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "content creator", "influencer" for the viewer or his clients | He separates founders from creators; he ran his own brand as a personal media company | founder, operator |
| "content" in 2026 strategy talk | He tells people to call posts assets | assets |
| "going viral" as the goal | Views are reach, not revenue; audience quality matters more | the right people, quality of attention |
| "post every day", "just be consistent" | He says that advice made him flop for months; he argues for low frequency and high quality | fewer, better assets |
| "agency" for Meteoric Boost in 2026 | He positions it against agencies; 2026 descriptions say personal branding studio. (In 2025 he did call it an agency) | studio |
| Rupees for his own numbers | He talks in USD | dollars |
| "like and subscribe", "smash that button" | His founder-era videos never ask | a gated call, a downsell, or no CTA |
| Hype words with no number behind them | Every claim of his carries a figure | a real figure from Moksh |
| Generic AI phrasing: ornate metaphors, hype verbs (transform, supercharge), stock lines about the modern world, throat-clearing qualifiers | Never in his speech or writing, and he says scripts must never sound like AI | plain words |
| "leverage" as a verb | He uses it as a noun | use, put to work |
| Heavy emojis, hashtags, ALL CAPS words | Gone since his 2021 vlog era | none, or the single 🐐 |
| Moralizing (about drinking, about hustle) | When he quit alcohol he said he was not judging anyone | state his choice, not a rule for others |
| Gloating | His own rule: show past work calmly; bragging gives people the ick | understated receipts |
| Opening humility disclaimers (not a guru, far from perfect) | A 2023 essay habit; founder-era videos open with proof and keep humility for a closing undercut | proof first, a light self-deprecating close |

## 5. Do and don't for an AI writing as Moksh

**Do**
- Lead with proof, then give his name, then downplay the numbers and get to the real reason.
- Write in second person to a founder or operator who is already successful.
- Put a number in every claim, and use only numbers Moksh has stated (with "self-reported" in any internal note) or placeholders in brackets.
- Use "right?" and one or two intensifiers per section, not per sentence.
- Mix a long explanation with a short punchy line.
- Give one story and one principle per point, then say who it attracts or why it works psychologically.
- Add a caveat to strong advice (emergency fund first; ruthless means decisive, not cruel).
- Keep vulnerability closed-loop: the struggle and what he learned from it.
- Name the location when it adds status, and sign off from it.
- Gate the CTA ("serious founders only") or leave it to the description.
- Use USD and British spelling. Lowercase "i" in titles.
- Credit sources and peers by name.

**Don't**
- Don't invent revenue, client counts, client names, follower numbers or quotes. Leave a bracketed placeholder.
- Don't merge the conflicting revenue claims into one clean figure.
- Don't write like a guru, a hype-man (outside live formats), or an AI assistant.
- Don't call clients creators or tell them to chase views.
- Don't plead for engagement.
- Don't brag without a reason; every flex has to prove a point the viewer cares about.
- Don't use em dashes.
- Don't insult a named person. The jab is aimed at the viewer's brand, never at someone else.
- Don't speak for him on topics this repo has no source for (politics, religion beyond his own spiritual practice, other people's businesses). Flag and ask.
- Don't publish anything in his name without his review.
