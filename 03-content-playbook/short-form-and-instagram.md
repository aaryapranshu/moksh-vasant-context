# Short-form and Instagram playbook

How Moksh Vasant (founder and CEO of Meteoric Boost, a personal branding studio for founders) runs Instagram and short-form video: account stats, what his 32 scripted Shorts actually do, his stated reel method, hooks, B-roll, formats, story sequences (including the 9-frame Peak Form sequence), the 3-second profile, and the playbook numbers that changed over time.

**What changed on 2026-10-20.** Full transcripts now exist for his podcasts, workshops and live builds, and for 43 YouTube Shorts (Sep 2024 to Jun 2025) that are almost certainly re-posts of his Instagram reels (inference: the batch includes a Father's Day reel, 0MPbEYQDK7Q [00:28], and p6Qg2wproJo opens with the exact breakout hook he recounts on The Sanskar Show). So for the first time the repo has **verbatim scripts of his reel-era formula**. Instagram itself is still unread (blocked to crawlers): no live caption, highlight or story was viewed directly.

## 3. The 2024 to 2025 Shorts: what his reel scripts actually do

From the 32 spoken Shorts (transcripts). These are the closest thing to his real reel scripts in the repo.

### 3.1 Length and pace
- 25 to 62 seconds; most 37 to 59. Three one-line aphorism Shorts run 12 to 25 seconds.
- About 145 to 260 words, roughly 4 words a second. Continuous voiceover over B-roll, no pauses. This matches his stated rule of a "flowing" script with clips "switching every second second and a half" (TSS [17:51]).
- 11 of the 43 Shorts are music-only edits with the idea in the title (e.g. "Be a paradox 🃏"). Do not quote their lyrics as his words.

**Algorithm model** (qYI [43:06] to [45:28], his own data, self-reported):
- "The only two metrics that matter on the algorithm is engagement rate and value." [43:06]
- Engagement rate = "reel interactions plus profile follows upon total views into 100" [43:41].
- Above 7.5% reaches "a fairly wide audience"; above 9% about 60% new viewers; above 11% about 90% new; "The minute you cross 14% hail Mary, like you're viral" [43:53] to [44:15].
- Fractional exposure: pushed to 5 active followers, then 50, 500, 5,000 [44:53]. Also H68: "engagement equals fractional exposure on the algorithm" [20:23].
- 80/20: three reels brought 18K, 25K and 45K followers, "about 90k followers out of like 137k" [44:15] to [44:27].
- Value: "inspiration, education, problem solving. That's like the three main things of value" [45:28], with motivation, aspiration and entertainment also named [45:16]. (Older versions of this file said "four kinds of value".)

## 5. Hook and B-roll rules

### 5.1 Hooks (TSS #226)
- "a good hook should be filmed in good sunlight... good outfit, right? And face is visible" [24:22] to [24:35].
- "you should play to your unfair advantage. If you're working with like [Dhanil, captioned 'Donald'], for example, his fitness coaching, his unfair advantage is his physique. So I'll get him to be shirtless in sunlight" [24:35].
- Say something polarizing: "as a 21-year-old building India's next unicorn" [25:14]. Money flexes: "For some people they want to do it, some people don't" [25:29].
- Script hooks for clients: a specific scene ("4:00 a.m. Paramount Hotel balcony...") or "the classic... as a 22-year-old or a 24-year-old or a 25-year-old" [30:19] to [30:32].
- Conflict and negative hooks: "I don't look like a typical beauty founder, but I'm building a skin care brand... and here's why" (qYI [22:10]); "negative hooks always work" [22:34].
- The age A/B test sometimes credited to him (a 24-year-old hook flopped, 23 worked) is Ujjwal's, a workshop attendee (qYI [38:41] to [39:39]).

### 5.2 Shot angles and the sunlight reserve (HPCsujvgIvE, Mar 2026)
- Side angle = "in my element"; front gaze = authority; from behind = "follow the leader psychology" [22:23] to [22:52].
- Training clips signal "pursuit of a superior self" [22:52]; an "occasional fun selfie" [23:17].
- He keeps a stock of sunlit-face shots because "I know that hook is going to work" [24:03].
- Starter pack of objects (Omega Seamaster, linen shirts, a black bag, a scent) [26:48] to [27:36]. In Bali: "linen pants, linen shirts, that's all I wear" (qSNY [30:45]).

### 5.3 B-roll categories (TSS)
Work (desk from behind, above or the side; calls; screens), lifestyle in motion (driving, dinner, walking, tying shoes), objects (shoes, steering wheel, wall textures), POV of the workspace, fitness. The psychology: life in motion signals the person is busy and worth following.

### 5.4 Colour
- **Five palettes, not four** (HPCs [30:58] to [32:44]): deep charcoal blacks with warm undertones; industrial/urban; warm desert/sunset; natural/tropical; universal blue. Match to emotion: intense video = industrial/urban plus charcoal plus warm desert; laid-back = tropical plus blue [32:56].
- Goal: "old money elegance plus young rich energy" (HPCs [28:47]).
- Test: "if they notice the grading, we have failed as a creative team" (HPCs [30:11]). He says avoid "orange or teal color wheels" [30:11] but his industrial palette uses teal shadows and orange highlights [31:17]; keep both, flag the tension.
- **Seasonality:** "right now it's winter, right? So a lot of my clips will be warmer, darker... the audios that I'll be using won't be as bright. I won't show a lot of like water"; in summer "a lot of blues... ocean blues" (TSS [28:06] to [28:31]). Same rule for his profile picture (0jNq [02:16]).

## 7. Content formats and their jobs

His rule: separate reach from leads. "If you're trying to get reach while trying to get leads, you're not going to solve either problems" (qYI [30:47]). Client default is three formats: "an informative format, a raw connection format as well as like a reach format" (qYI [31:11]). If the only goal is clients, one format is enough (qYI [29:25]).

|---|---|---|---|

| ICP video | Trust with buyers; views do not matter | "videos that generate trust in your ICP or ideal customer profiles mind" | BU0 [11:28] |
| Raw documentation | Connection | "just sharing your thoughts in a raw format which just builds trust" | BU0 [11:28] |

**Correction:** earlier versions said his reach format was "whiteboard explainers". The caption "white time" means **wide TAM**. "Board videos" are a separate long-form lead format.

**Middle of funnel is problem content:** "Pain point, value. Pain point, value. Pain point, value." (TSS [22:37]). "There's so many like maybe 10, 20 problems. Now you have 10, 20 solutions to those problems. And all of it put together is your offer" (TSS [21:19] to [21:31]).

**Formats by funnel stage** (live build for a PE client, AnqK [1:09:50] to [1:22:42]): top = reporter/curation, series, storytelling, 3-second edits with text and a long caption ("99 [percent] of content is either creation or curation", [1:11:07]); middle = one direct problem-solution format, talking head or split screen ("If they feel like you're talking to them and they feel hurt that's the nail in the coffin", [1:20:25]); stories bridge middle to bottom [1:10:12]; bottom = DMs plus YouTube links.

**The perception stack** (Bali talk, qSNY [32:21] to [34:31]): Moksh's own content, team content, testimonials (clipped with B-roll, emotional music, Discord screenshots), the Meteoric page, and 2 to 3 story sequences a week (Q&A, value, curiosity). Team members post their own videos about scaling Meteoric; the company page posts client results almost daily, "super low effort, but it's super high reward" (BU0 [13:22] to [14:13], [17:27]).

**Follower threshold** (Daksh's build): wide-TAM content until about 10K followers, then story sequences with CTAs (C4ckCV1xmj8 [08:05]).

**Bulk and cut** (credited to Saul): alternate reach phases and conversion phases.

## 9. The profile: the 3-second profile (Aug 2026)

From "how i'd structure a millionaire's instagram profile (live breakdown)" (0jNq7A4_yOM, published 23 Aug 2026, not September).

- **The test:** "You get literally 3 seconds when a cold person lands on your Instagram profile and that's your entire first impression" [00:00]. A cold visitor "cannot tell in seconds who you are, what you do, or who it is for. So, they leave with no impression at all, which is worse than having a bad impression" [00:59]. He calls it "the 3-second profile": the visitor "asks four questions in this exact sequence and every surface exists to answer" one of them [01:37] to [01:50]. The fourth, asked at the highlights, is "is this built for someone like me?" [04:46]. He never lists all four in one sentence; the list is assembled from [00:59] and [04:46].
- **Frame:** "a modern-day landing page" [01:37]; "Your profile is the product demo" [12:40]; "You don't argue credibility. You simply stack it and display it in front of a cold audience" [12:27].
- **Profile picture:** "clean, high authority... face is visible... zoomed in and you're in good lighting"; he changes it "three to four times every single year", matched to season [02:02] to [02:29].
- **Bio:** "founder and CEO of [@meteoric.boost]"; "soon [@Asava, captioned] event... this events company with my business partner"; tagline "I work with founders to build the top 0.1% personal brands to inspire movements" [03:07] to [03:20].
