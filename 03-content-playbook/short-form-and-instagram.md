# Short-form and Instagram playbook

What is known about how Moksh Vasant (founder of Meteoric Boost, a personal brand studio for founders) runs Instagram and short-form video: account stats, his viral reel formula, hook, B-roll, script and retention rules (mostly from The Sanskar Show #226), his content formats, story sequences, carousels, profile structure, the Bamboo Grow series, and what could not be accessed.

**Important limit:** Instagram itself could not be read (blocked to crawlers). No reel, post, caption or highlight was viewed directly. Everything here comes from what Moksh says about his short-form method in videos, podcasts and his newsletter, plus third-party stat tools. Treat it as his stated method, not an audit of his actual posts.

## 3. The viral reel formula (his own stated method)

From The Sanskar Show #226 (Mar 2026), cross-checked with his Sep 2025 workshop (qYIKwqRvIyU):

| Element | His rule |
|---|---|
| Hook | Broad, built on age plus a flex or identity, so many people can project onto it. Polarizing or surprising claims work |
| First visual | A pattern interrupt: fast visual change, zoom or camera move |
| Cuts | Every 1 to 1.5 seconds |
| Audio | House music at about 120 to 140 BPM that builds (H68 says 140 to 180 BPM for setting the viewer's state; the two figures differ) |
| Script | Flowing, with no dead air between lines |
| Length | About 60 seconds |
| Posting time | About 30 minutes before the audience's peak, if an audience exists. For his mostly Indian audience: Friday to Sunday around 8pm IST (qYI) |
| Frequency | Two posts a week at most (H68); low frequency, high quality |
| Skeleton | Hook, Authority, Flow, CTA (qYI): hook wide, establish credibility right away, tell the journey or lesson, then a keyword CTA |

His own breakout hook, as he recounted it on The Sanskar Show: "as a 20-year-old, clarity of thought is my superpower."

**Diagnosing a weak reel** (qYI): check the hook, the lighting, the dynamics, and the speed of clip switching, in that order.

**Algorithm model** (qYI, his own data, self-reported):
- Engagement rate = (likes + saves + shares + DMs + comments + follows from that viewer) divided by views, times 100.
- Above about 7.5% gets wide distribution; above 9% means about 60% new viewers; above 11% about 90%; above 14% viral.
- Distribution expands in stages of about 10x as each batch validates the content.
- 80/20: three reels brought about 90K of his 137K followers.
- Four kinds of value: inspiration, education, problem-solving, entertainment.

## 4. Hook rules for reels

From The Sanskar Show #226 unless noted:
- It must stop the scroll. If the first four or five clips land, the video works.
- Good sunlight. Dark footage loses.
- A good outfit with the face clearly visible.
- Show the person's unfair advantage first (a fitness coach leads with his physique).
- A surprising or polarizing claim (his example: a 21-year-old building a unicorn).
- Money flexes can work, but only for some people.
- Two staple hook types for scripts: a vivid, specific scene (a 4 a.m. hotel balcony with millionaires) or a proven identity and age hook.
- Conflict or negative hooks work too (qYI): a founder who does not look like their category; "most creators are broke".
- Age A/B lesson (qYI): a hook using an older age felt too old for his audience and underperformed; one year younger did better.

## 5. B-roll rules

From The Sanskar Show #226, plus the shot library in "High status aesthetics to attract the top 1% (breakdown)" (HPCsujvgIvE, about Apr 2026):

**B-roll categories (Sanskar Show):**
- Work: desk from behind, above or the side; calls and meetings; screens
- Lifestyle in motion: driving, dinner, walking, tying shoes
- Objects: shoes, steering wheel, tyre, wall texture close-ups
- POV of the workspace
- Fitness

**Shot library (HPCs):** side angles, front gaze shots, follow-from-behind shots, sunlit hero shots, POV and lifestyle shots, fitness shown as a hobby, focused work shots.

**The psychology:** documenting life in motion signals the person is always busy and worth following. Show life in motion, not posing (HPCs).

**Color:**
- Mix tones for a clean look. Taste varies: warm oranges and yellows, or dark, cold and underexposed. He goes warmer and darker in winter, bright and ocean-blue in tropical places (Sanskar Show).
- Target "old money meets young rich", natural grades with no visible filter. Palettes matched to the emotion of each video: dark industrial, warm desert, tropical sage and teal, universal blue. The test is that viewers think the person simply looks good, not that they notice the grade (HPCs).

**Visual identity principles (HPCs):** less is more; make effort look effortless (sprezzatura); flex taste, not money; color-coordinate the frame; curate the whole frame, not just the subject; post for one specific person. Build a mood board of the world first (he says he built one for the life he wanted before he could afford it), then shoot into it. The brand works like a "starter pack" of recognizable objects and places. Everything is tailored to each client's taste.

## 6. Scripts and retention for reels

- **He writes his own scripts** because the style is his (Sanskar Show). For clients, his team built an internal AI workflow on Claude that uses the context gathered in the first weeks plus a custom prompt. The rule: it must never sound like AI.
- **His personal scripting process** (Epiphanies #15, May 2025): journal nightly and weekly, pull 5 to 6 recurring themes, brain-dump each, shape into hook, body and outro, batch-record 6 to 7 voiceovers, hand to the editing team in Notion, one weekly review call. About 2 to 3 hours a month for about 12 reels (self-reported).
- **Backstory as raw material** (qYI): write a 1,500 to 2,000 word unfiltered autobiography (voice notes transcribed with AI are fine), pull out 5 or 6 pivotal moments ("parables") and weave them into scripts.
- **Retention checklist** (Sanskar Show): visuals, script flow and timing aligned; fast-switching visuals; audio that builds; a slightly unusual hook; a flowing script; about 60 seconds; post about 30 minutes before peak.
- **Production system** (qYI, H68): an editor plus a creative strategist or social manager; batch the month in about 2 days (one filming, one scripting and voiceovers) for about 6 to 8 videos; Notion or a sheet for the pipeline, a Google Drive B-roll library, WhatsApp for comms; a 30 to 45 minute weekly call. Founder time 4 to 8 hours a month.

## 7. Content formats and their jobs

His rule: one format, one job. Run 2 or 3 formats, each mapped to reach, leads or connection. Views only matter for the reach format.

| Format | Job | Notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B-roll plus voiceover plus house music | Reach | One reached about 3.2 to 3.4M views and about 45K followers (self-reported) | qYI |
| Whiteboard explainers / ICP value reels | Leads and trust with buyers | Views do not matter for this one | qYI, BU0 |
| Raw talking-to-camera documentation | Connection | Example: a video at 100K followers saying numbers do not matter | qYI |
| "Edits" | Fun | Color-graded, music-synced aspirational clips, often made on Sundays | qYI |
| Carousels | Explain the mechanism; pinned for specific segments | Pattern interrupt, then value, then CTA | qYI, BU0 |
| Story sequences | Conversion | See section 8 | qYI, BU0 |

**Bulk and cut** (an idea he credits to a peer, Saul): alternate reach phases and conversion phases. If traffic is fine but conversion is weak, add ICP reels (for example two a week).

**Client formats** (case study sD4R8Z4H6U4, Mar 2026): two pillars, storytelling first and lead magnets second; raw talking-head and in-car videos; lifestyle set pieces; proof content. The studio offer lists 3 formats per founder (meteoricboost.com).

## 9. The profile as a landing page

From "how i'd structure a millionaire's instagram profile (live breakdown)" (0jNq7A4_yOM, about Sep 2026) and BU0 (Jan 2026):
- **Three-second test.** A cold visitor must be able to answer, in order: who you are, what you do, who it is for, and whether it is for someone like them. A credible but illegible founder leaves no impression, which he says is worse than a weak one.
- **Profile picture:** tight, well lit, face clearly visible. He refreshes his a few times a year.
- **Bio:** role and company, what he does, a tagline, a "DM me [keyword]" line (BU0), and a link to a long pre-sell video about how he built his brand.
- **Highlights**, each built to answer one hesitation: life, thoughts and mental models, an ideal-client reel, a "start here" series of about 10 to 15 stories, client proof. Earlier versions: Start Here and Clients (H68); starter, Q&A, team, testimonials (BU0).
- **Three pinned posts:** a client testimonial edit, a team-culture video, a personal intro (0jNq). In BU0 the pins were two carousels for two segments (people who want recognition; info-product owners who want to monetize).
- **Grid:** one visual theme. His is motion (travel, cars, a helicopter, suits). Each client gets their own.
- **Why:** every surface targets the same high-caliber person with no contradictions. Prospects audit every channel in a "two-day research window" before reaching out, so each surface has to convert (Oktoberfest video, Oct 2026).

## 10. Funnel: where Instagram sits

1. Top: reels for reach and curiosity.
2. Middle: value and ICP content that names a specific problem, then gives value. YouTube long-form as the trust asset (he sends long videos in DMs as nurture).
3. Bottom: story sequences, a DM keyword, an appointment setter, a discovery call, a pre-sell asset, a closing call.

He compares it to meeting someone in real life: curiosity, then trust, then commitment (Sanskar Show). "DM is the only funnel" was his line in Jan 2026 (BU0): no website, no VSL at that point. His 2026 site is now part of the trust layer.

**Small accounts can still sell** (Sanskar Show): a short list of high-value followers, trust content, an optimized profile, a 20 to 30 minute pre-sell video, an appointment setter sending 50 to 100 targeted DMs a day, and tracking open, reply, conversation, booked, show-up and close rates.

## 11. Captions and on-platform copy

**Not directly observed.** No Instagram caption was readable. What can be inferred from his other written surfaces (YouTube titles and descriptions, newsletter):
- Lowercase "i", short lines, very few emojis (the 🐐 is his one recurring emoji, on the description tagline).
- Short CTAs built on a keyword ("DM METEORIC").
- Give-first framing (free value, paid implementation).
- No hashtags in founder-era YouTube copy; early vlogs used them.

When writing a caption for him, keep it to a few lines, open on the tension or result, end on one keyword CTA or none, and flag it as a draft for Moksh to check against his real captions.
